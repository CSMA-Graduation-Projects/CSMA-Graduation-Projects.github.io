# Research task card

Purpose: turn one stage objective in undergraduate or master's research into a small task that can be started, submitted and assessed. Suitable for initial reading, reproduction, data checks, prototype development or problem validation. “Become familiar with a field” is not sufficient as the only task.

Instructions: address one main question per card. Match the objective to the student's background, courses and resources. Define the minimum deliverable before optional extensions. For example, “Run a baseline on a small authorized sample and explain one failure case” is more assessable than “Learn large language models.”

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Explain the result | [Check the output and one failure case] | [Observations, questions, next-step suggestions] | [Do claims stay within the evidence?] | [Complete] |

Optional extensions: [Work to begin only after the minimum objective is accepted; extensions are not mandatory by default].

## 3. Resources, blockers and help

- Required resources: [Data permission, software, equipment access and estimated run cost; do not record secrets].
- Risks and ethics: [Personal data, external services, unpublished material and prerequisite approvals].
- When blocked: record [Steps to reproduce, error message, attempted solutions and a minimal example], then ask a specific question.
- Review points: [What to submit and when; when to explain the impact if a deadline cannot be met].
- Evidence labels: distinguish live runs, authors' results, saved-record replay and constructed examples. Work that has not run cannot be marked “Reproduced.”

- [ ] Suggestions follow from the findings and the task has not expanded indefinitely.
