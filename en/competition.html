How to use this pathway

Eligibility, format, fees and award recognition may change between editions. An award does not automatically replace course or degree requirements. Check rules, permissions and contributions before reusing a project.

Suggested timing helps you plan; official deadlines come from the notices applicable to you. Complete the institutional-requirements check, then agree on submission and feedback dates for each stage.

LEARN / PRACTISE / REFLECT

Learning and growth

What you will learn

  • Extract eligibility, scoring criteria, restrictions and submission conditions from the current rules, then turn the project objectives into checkable team tasks.
  • Coordinate responsibilities, integration, testing and backup arrangements within limited time and resources, recording individual contributions and shared interfaces.
  • Demonstrate the actual work under the required conditions, explain failures and trade-offs, and derive transferable lessons from judging feedback and process records.

Check your starting point

  • Provide the organizer's rules for the current event and its registration window, then complete an eligibility and scoring checklist. For ambiguous clauses, attach the official inquiry record and its current status.
  • Each member demonstrates an existing project or small exercise relevant to their proposed role and provides availability after course and research commitments. Record how missing roles will be covered.
  • Test the dependency most likely to fail, recording actual results, permissions and resource costs. A component that cannot be used in the competition environment needs a verifiable alternative.

Your responsibility

The team lead maintains dependencies, integration dates and submission status. Members own their deliverables and understand the shared demonstration path. Report the impact and alternatives when interfaces change, critical defects emerge, members leave or rules are unclear. Follow the event's required sequence for registration and submission; do not miss a registration window while waiting for a complete prototype.

Guidance and support

The mentor helps assess direction, rule boundaries and high-risk decisions. A member who did not implement the relevant module checks its instructions and interfaces. Authorized people confirm funding, permissions and team changes through the appropriate procedures. Keep advice separate from official eligibility and result decisions.

Feedback and revision

At integration checkpoints, actually run the candidate version against the scoring criteria, recording failures, owners and review dates. Another member then reruns the critical path. After the event, compare judging feedback with existing tests and choose a concrete improvement. Record awards separately; ranking does not replace analysis of individual learning or project quality.

Evidence for the next step

Before continuing as research, a capstone or a maintained product, distinguish competition features, research hypotheses and engineering responsibilities, then establish the new question, evaluation and individual tasks. Reuse existing work with permission and add evidence required by the new setting. An award does not automatically establish a thesis contribution or satisfy course or degree requirements.

01

Before registration or equipment purchases

Competition, eligibility and rules

Confirm that the competition is worthwhile, that you can enter, and that the commitment fits your learning goals.

Before you start

  • You have the organizer's official notice and rules for the current edition.
  • You understand applicable institutional registration, recognition or nomination procedures.

What to do

  • Verify the organizer, track, year/enrollment eligibility, team size, cross-institution restrictions, supervisor requirements and schedule.
  • Extract project-format requirements, scoring criteria and restrictions on originality, repeat entries, public data and AI tools.
  • Assess potential benefits, timetable conflicts, fees, equipment and computing needs. Compare whether another task or competition would be a better fit.
  • Ask official channels about ambiguous rules and retain responses. Complete any required institutional nomination, seal or registration approval procedures first.

What to submit

  • A rules summary, original links and verification date.
  • An eligibility self-check, competition schedule and initial budget.
  • A participation decision and applicable institutional approval records.

How completion is checked

  • Every eligibility condition is met; rules from a past edition are not used as authority for the current one.
  • Core scoring criteria and restrictions have been translated into task conditions.
  • Costs and timing are manageable, and the official registration route is clear.
If requirements are not met

If ineligible, change track or stop registration. Avoid irreversible investment in high-risk matters with unclear rules until the organizer responds.

02

Before team registration and development

Team, topic and ownership agreement

Establish a shared understanding of the problem, roles, commitment, authorship and rights to use the work.

Before you start

  • Competition eligibility and rules are verified.
  • Prospective members understand timetable conflicts and their available time.

What to do

  • Assign a team lead and responsibilities for development, research, design and presentation, including backups. Assign tasks to individuals rather than only to subgroups.
  • Choose a real problem suited to the track. Specify user needs, core innovation, the minimal project and its mapping to scoring criteria.
  • List existing code, data, models, images and collaborative resources. Confirm licenses, permitted use and whether ethics or confidentiality approval is required.
  • Discuss contribution records, author order, applicable reward-distribution rules and withdrawal/handover arrangements. Handle institutional or partner ownership under formal policies.

What to submit

  • Team details, responsibilities and communication agreements.
  • A project brief, scoring-criteria mapping and milestones.
  • A resource-provenance, ownership and permission list.

How completion is checked

  • Every member confirms their tasks and commitment, and team registration meets the rules.
  • The scope is executable and individual contributions are traceable.
  • Rights to use materials and outputs are clear. No one has privately promised intellectual property they lack authority to transfer.
If requirements are not met

If roles or ownership remain disputed, pause the affected development or publication. Process membership changes under competition procedures and reconfirm responsibilities.

03

Check the official registration window separately; validate high-risk assumptions before committing major resources.

Prototype, budget and registration

Demonstrate feasibility with a minimal prototype while completing registration and resource preparation.

Before you start

  • Responsibilities, project scope and ownership boundaries are confirmed.
  • Confirm registration fields, attachments, payment and institutional approvals. Treat the official deadline as an independent milestone; registration and prototyping may overlap or occur in a different order under the rules.

What to do

  • Test the most failure-prone technical or data element first. Build a prototype that demonstrates the core value, recording success and failure conditions.
  • Detail the equipment, computing, travel and registration budget. Check reimbursement rules and approval authority; do not treat unconfirmed funding as available.
  • Prepare the registration form, declarations and applicable attachments. Check member names, institutions, supervisors and track details item by item.
  • Register through the official channel within its window and retain the receipt and review status. If a complete prototype is not required, do not wait for internal prototype review before registering; if it is required, plan backward to meet it. Track corrections and later deadlines.

What to submit

  • A prototype, feasibility evidence and revision plan.
  • A detailed budget and applicable approval records.
  • Registration materials, receipts and review-status records.

How completion is checked

  • The prototype answers the critical feasibility question; a concept diagram is not described as an implementation.
  • Costs have identified funding sources and owners, and purchases are authorized.
  • Registration has actually been submitted or approved, with verifiable status evidence.
If requirements are not met

If the prototype fails, change approach before the deadline. Correct returned applications according to feedback. Stop further losses and record the decision if eligibility or funding issues cannot be resolved.

04

Before formal submission, allowing time for fixes

Implementation, replication and internal acceptance

Make the project run reliably in the prescribed environment and explain the source of every claimed score or performance result.

Before you start

  • Project requirements and the scoring mapping are frozen as the baseline for this cycle.
  • The team has working version control and a test environment.

What to do

  • Implement against scoring criteria and maintain task status, recording contributions, versions and changes. Integrate regularly instead of merging everything near the deadline.
  • Test normal, boundary and failure scenarios on the required equipment or conditions. Check restrictions on networking, external services and data use.
  • Have a member who did not implement a module reproduce it from the instructions. Check dependencies, licenses, data paths, runtime and demonstration reliability.
  • Complete manuals, posters, videos or presentation slides. Distinguish implemented prototype features from planned features, then freeze a submission candidate after item-by-item acceptance.

What to submit

  • A runnable project, dependencies and operating instructions.
  • Testing/replication records, a defect list and contribution records.
  • Candidate submission materials and an internal acceptance checklist.

How completion is checked

  • Scoring criteria map to evidence, and unimplemented features are not claimed as complete.
  • The project can be reproduced in the agreed environment, with failures and limitations disclosed.
  • Code, data, video and manuals use consistent versions, and AI use complies with the current rules.
If requirements are not met

If critical defects cannot be fixed, remove unreliable features and update the description. Replace components that violate the rules and retest; do not conceal dependencies.

05

Follow the official deadline, time zone and session

Submission, competition and confirmed results

Submit the correct version, keep valid evidence and follow the rules for demonstrations and appeals.

Before you start

  • The submission candidate has passed acceptance, with confirmation from all required members or supervisors.
  • File sizes, names, formats, upload limits and the final deadline are verified.

What to do

  • Check package contents, sensitive information, permission statements and member details. Record the final version and checksum, allowing time to resolve upload failures.
  • After uploading, download or preview the files to check the contents. Save the submission identifier, time, receipt and platform status.
  • Demonstrate the work as required, recording the version and judges' questions. Explain unexpected failures accurately; do not present edited footage as a live execution.
  • Use official notices to confirm progression, awards and certificate details. Request a review of disputed results through the required channel, within the deadline and with the required evidence.

What to submit

  • The final submission package, version record and receipt.
  • On-site materials, question records and correction records where applicable.
  • Official results or certificates, and review decisions where applicable.

How completion is checked

  • Submission status is verifiable, with no accidentally uploaded old versions or empty files.
  • The demonstration follows the rules, and the team knows the next notification and correction windows.
  • Award names, levels and members match the official record, without inferring recognition from predictions or screenshots.
If requirements are not met

If upload fails, use the organizer's emergency channel immediately and retain evidence. Raise disputes through the formal process; do not alter results, certificates or public claims yourself.

06

After the competition ends or withdrawal is agreed

Reflection, expense settlement and archiving

Turn competition experience into resources for continued learning and use, while completing team responsibilities.

Before you start

  • Submission, result and expense records for the current round are collected.
  • Applicable institutional recognition and reimbursement procedures are confirmed.

What to do

  • Compare goals, scoring criteria and actual results to identify strengths and mistakes. Distinguish technical, organizational, presentation and rule-interpretation issues.
  • Check receipts, cost-sharing, equipment and borrowed resources. Complete reimbursement and settlement as required; estimates cannot replace actual documents.
  • Archive the project, reports, rules version, submission receipts, result evidence and individual contributions. Decide who will maintain the system or arrange shutdown.
  • Confirm rules and permissions for later open-source release, patents, papers or capstone reuse. Use official status and authorized materials in public presentations.

What to submit

  • A reflection report, follow-up actions and individual contribution statement.
  • An archive index, final project and official result evidence.
  • Expense settlement, equipment return and handover records.

How completion is checked

  • The team can reuse lessons learned, with concrete improvements for key problems.
  • Funding, equipment, accounts and unfinished tasks all have recorded dispositions.
  • Output recognition follows official institutional decisions, and publication scope and intellectual property are confirmed.
If requirements are not met

Mark missing receipts, permissions or unconfirmed results as outstanding tasks with owners. Do not fabricate closure evidence or describe participation itself as an award.
