Day-to-day coordination gives each task an owner, brings difficulties to light and turns feedback into a next step. First agree with your supervisor on meeting frequency, where to keep materials and how feedback will be provided, then adapt the arrangement to your coursework and research. This page offers working methods. Attendance, leave, practical training, funding and assessment follow the applicable university and department rules.

Agree on responsibilities and communication

Students are responsible for understanding requirements, proposing plans, carrying out the work, keeping accurate records and reporting difficulties promptly. Supervisors guide direction, methods, resources and quality; explain feedback arrangements; and help assess scope and priorities. Formal program approvals, recognition of research outputs and degree applications are handled by the designated university staff and bodies. A successful group review indicates readiness for the next step; it cannot replace formal approval.

On joining the group, agree on the term's goals, regular meeting times, material to prepare in advance, communication channels, expected feedback times, a contact for urgent matters and leave procedures. Adjust the rhythm to the task. This website does not prescribe uniform daily working hours or treat publication counts, time online or message response speed as assessment criteria without an applicable agreed basis.

Connect daily work to the term's goals

At the start of a working day, choose the most important deliverable and check its inputs and resources. At the end, record completed work, file versions, experiment status and the next step. A failed run can also be informative: record the expectation, observation, causes already investigated and next check. Repeated attempts alone do not establish progress.

Each week, prepare a brief report against the plan with accessible evidence and update task status after feedback. Each month, revisit whether the research question still holds, the work remains on track and the resource budget is sufficient; demonstrate completed milestones through a runnable or verifiable result. Each term, check coursework, academic activities, professional practice and formal assessments against your program requirements, and record the next official deadlines in advance.

These intervals are suggested starting points. During intensive coursework, an internship, concentrated experiments or defense preparation, agree on a revised schedule with your supervisor and a clear next review date. Avoid committing to several tasks that compete for the same time. Establish the key task first, then schedule supporting reading and tool learning.

Manage tasks through completion criteria

A task should specify the problem, deliverable, review method, owner and review date. For example, replace “continue experiments” with “run the agreed data split and baseline, then submit commands, configuration, complete logs and an explanation of result differences.” A delivery date is a plan; completed status must be supported by material and review findings.

When the topic, data, method or deadline changes substantially, preserve the original plan and explain the reason, how completed work will be used, the impact and the revised plan. Obtain confirmation from the relevant person. If the deadline becomes infeasible, report the difficulty early and reduce scope; do not fabricate progress after the deadline.

Make reports and meetings lead to action

For collaboration across languages, agree on meeting and feedback languages, share terminology and topics in advance, and confirm understanding with a short action record afterward. Do not use spoken fluency as a substitute for assessing research quality. See support and communication for international students for language, learning and resource support.

A progress report covers the original weekly plan, actual work and evidence, what was learned, unresolved questions and next week's deliverables. Separate facts from explanations: “two of three runs failed” is an observation; “insufficient resources may be responsible” is an explanation to test. A reading update should explain what you understood and how it relates to the current question. An implementation update should identify verified features and work still incomplete.

Send materials at the agreed time before the meeting and list specific questions. Prioritise the evidence behind conclusions, important difficulties and choices between approaches. Afterwards, record decisions, actions, owners, review dates and evidence to add. Mark unresolved matters as pending confirmation; do not record an assumption as a supervisor's decision. Begin the next discussion by reviewing previous actions before moving to new questions.

When submitting a draft to your supervisor, identify the version, main changes, requested scope of feedback and planned submission date, allowing reasonable review time. If feedback is later than agreed, send a brief reminder identifying the affected milestone. If an official deadline is at risk, also contact the responsible department staff to seek a workable arrangement.

Distinguish leave, internships and required professional practice

Leave changes study or attendance arrangements for a specified period. An internship is a work or project experience within or outside the university. Professional practice is a component specified by a program's curriculum. An internship does not automatically fulfil that component, and an employer's certificate does not automatically replace university assessment. Before departure, check who must receive the application, the approval order, insurance or safety requirements, confidentiality and ownership of outputs, and effects on coursework and research milestones.

An application should give dates, arrangements, effects on tasks, continuity plans and necessary contact channels, and should be submitted through the designated controlled channel. Share sensitive health information only with authorised personnel. Before practical training, agree on learning goals, supervisors, tasks and assessment materials. Keep accurate records during the placement, submit the required report and evaluations afterwards, and mark the program component complete only after formal university recognition.

Estimate resources before scheduling experiments

Before requesting a server, model API, dataset or conference funding, state the purpose, expected duration or request volume, spending limit, data permissions and how resources will be released afterwards. Start with a small validation run before scaling up. Record resource usage and costs; stop expanding the run and discuss the situation if spending departs from the budget. Book shared equipment as agreed, and leave the purpose and stopping conditions for long-running tasks so others know how to handle them.

Follow institutional procedures for expenditure and reimbursement and retain compliant receipts. Before a collaboration starts, agree on permitted data use, authorship and intellectual property. An internship or competition does not itself authorise taking an organisation's data, code or unpublished results outside that organisation.

Support, wellbeing and corrections to records

If persistent obstacles, excessive workload or physical or mental health affect your studies, speak early with your supervisor, student adviser or university support service and agree on task adjustments and a review date. Questions about supervision, assessment or rights can be raised through the consultation, feedback or appeal channels published by your institution. Applicable rules determine the procedure. Evaluation should focus on work and evidence while protecting personal privacy.

AI may help summarise meetings and reports, provided the materials may be shared with the chosen tool. Check the resulting facts, names, dates and decisions yourself and remove unsupported inferences. Ask the relevant people to confirm decisions that concern them. AI-generated conclusions about attendance or progress must not directly determine assessment. When correcting a record, retain the explanation, date and person confirming the correction so later readers know which version is valid.
