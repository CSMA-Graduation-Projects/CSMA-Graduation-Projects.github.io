How to use this pathway

Academic master's programs emphasize academic research capability. Professional master's programs also address professional practice and application. Specific outputs and standards depend on the discipline or professional degree category. This page does not set institutional output counts, publication levels, screening percentages or deadlines.

Suggested timing helps you plan; official deadlines come from the notices applicable to you. Complete the institutional-requirements check, then agree on submission and feedback dates for each stage.

LEARN / PRACTISE / REFLECT

Learning and growth

What you will learn

  • Use evidence from real problems and related research to formulate a bounded, testable and feasible question, explaining its relationship to the closest work.
  • Independently design and conduct research or practice-based validation suited to the question, checking controls, data quality and contrary evidence and explaining where the findings apply.
  • Organize evidence into a coherent thesis, manage training requirements, feedback, achievement status and handover, and progressively develop independent, evidence-based judgment.

Check your starting point

  • Provide your applicable training plan and a checklist of courses, practice, academic activities and degree conditions, with official sources, gaps and contacts for clarification. Check achievement requirements at entry or topic selection, rather than waiting until the thesis is complete.
  • Compare a relevant set of closely related studies, citing original passages and distinguishing author-reported results, your own reproductions and unverified interpretations.
  • Submit a baseline reproduction, engineering problem analysis or feasibility experiment with actual versions, data sources, results and failures. Use it to propose a plan for missing skills and a next review date.

Your responsibility

Lead the collection of topic evidence, research planning and scheduling. Before discussions, submit your judgment, alternatives and decisions that need input. Seek review when evidence repeatedly contradicts a hypothesis, research boundaries change, or resources or formal requirements threaten the plan; propose a narrower scope, additional evidence or a revised approach.

Guidance and support

The supervisor advises on research positioning, methods and quality, helps identify critical assumptions and risks, and agrees on feasible review arrangements. Professional-degree students also confirm practice supervision and institutionally recognized assessment requirements. Peer reproduction and group discussion contribute evidence; formal training and degree reviews follow institutional procedures.

Feedback and revision

At topic, proposal and midterm checkpoints, review whether the question, method and evidence remain aligned and whether the student can explain the choices made. Turn comments into revision and validation tasks. When new evidence changes the conclusion, revisit positioning or design rather than hide counterexamples to preserve the original plan. Research and writing develop together, and important results are checked again before final submission.

Evidence for the next step

For doctoral training, identify a valuable unresolved question emerging from the completed work, then develop the case for original contribution, longer-term feasibility and systematic evidence. For professional work, transfer the research methods to requirements, validation and communication under real constraints. Existing publications or practice outcomes are starting points, not automatic satisfaction of admission or training requirements at the next level.

01

Complete after enrollment as required by the study plan; review each semester

Enrollment, study plan and working agreements

Clarify the program type, compulsory components, communication responsibilities and information management, establishing a sustainable research rhythm.

Before you start

  • Your degree category, field, program length and supervisory arrangements are confirmed.
  • You have the study plan applicable to you and access to departmental notices.

What to do

  • List courses, credits, practice, academic activities, annual review, proposal and degree milestones. Check outcome types, authorship, timing and recognition rules at entry; record the applicable document version, scope and unresolved requirements.
  • Prepare your individual study plan. Professional master's students should check practice placements, duration, internal/external supervision and assessment; academic master's students should check disciplinary training requirements.
  • Agree on the purposes of weekly reports, group meetings, email and the task system, including feedback frequency and how to seek help. Handle leave, travel and attendance under policies currently in force.
  • Set up research directories, version control, backups and access permissions, and complete integrity, ethics and safety training. Report health issues or major difficulties promptly and agree on adjustments.

What to submit

  • An individual study plan and institutional-requirements checklist.
  • A semester plan, skills-development list and communication agreement.
  • A materials directory, backup plan and applicable training records.

How completion is checked

  • The formal plan is confirmed as required, and compulsory components and responsible people are clear.
  • The student leads the work, with clear supervisory guidance and feedback responsibilities.
  • Missing attendance or reporting entries are checked and may be corrected. An automated summary cannot by itself establish misconduct.
If requirements are not met

If credit, practice or scheduling conflicts arise, consult the academic office promptly and revise the plan. Mark unconfirmed requirements as pending; do not assume another student's conditions apply to you.

02

Before proposal review, after necessary preliminary tests

Exploration and topic selection

Choose a question supported by real evidence, with a clear personal contribution and feasible resource and time requirements.

Before you start

  • You understand the field's foundations, existing projects and available resources.
  • You can access representative literature and lawful preliminary-validation materials.

What to do

  • Propose topics from practical problems, limitations of existing research or professional needs. Describe where the problem occurs and the supporting evidence.
  • Compare value, novelty, related methods, testability, resources and risks. Distinguish actual observations from constructed illustrations.
  • Run a small baseline replication or feasibility experiment, defining success, failure and stopping conditions. An idea alone does not establish feasibility.
  • Discuss the main question, subquestions and contribution boundaries with your supervisor. Professional master's students should also check the practice setting, applied outputs and partner permissions.

What to submit

  • A candidate-topic comparison and topic statement.
  • Preliminary replication/experiment records and a resource-risk table.
  • Question boundaries, intended contribution and a decision to continue or adjust.

How completion is checked

  • The problem demonstrably exists or is expressed as a testable hypothesis, and its value has supporting sources.
  • Individual work is distinguishable from the team's existing outputs.
  • Data, equipment, funding and time support a minimum feasible study.
If requirements are not met

If real evidence or critical resources are missing, narrow the question, change methods or change topic. Preserve negative results and discuss again before committing to large experiments.

03

Draft before proposal review; update throughout the research

Literature review and research positioning

Identify established findings, disagreements and your research entry point through traceable searching and comparison.

Before you start

  • You have a candidate question and search scope.
  • Relevant databases, papers and necessary original materials are accessible.

What to do

  • Define keywords, synonyms, date ranges, databases and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Save search dates and screening records.
  • Read core work closely and build a matrix covering questions, assumptions, methods, data, evaluation, results and limitations. Trace key original references.
  • Check how papers relate to their code, data and later revisions. Describe work you have not run as reported literature, not local replication.
  • Organize the review by questions and methods, explaining conflicting evidence, untested conditions and how your study contributes, rather than stitching together paper summaries.

What to submit

  • Search records, a reference collection and screening rationale.
  • Core reading notes, a comparison matrix and a map of research questions.
  • A review draft, research positioning and a list of missing evidence.

How completion is checked

  • Representative methods and contrary evidence are considered, and citations trace to original texts.
  • Claims such as not previously studied state their search boundaries and avoid unsupported absolutes.
  • The research positioning explains differences from the closest work and how those differences will be tested.
If requirements are not met

If important related work was missed, extend the search and rewrite the positioning. If the main question is already solved, change the question rather than inventing novelty through terminology.

04

Within the institution's prescribed proposal-review window

Research plan and formal proposal review

Develop an executable research plan and obtain an official outcome from the proposal-review process.

Before you start

  • The topic, review and preliminary feasibility materials are available.
  • Proposal eligibility, timing, review format and submission templates are checked.

What to do

  • Break the main question into testable subquestions, assigning methods, baselines, data, metrics and expected evidence to each.
  • Prepare the schedule, computing and funding budget, ethics/data-permission arrangements, and alternatives for failure, delay or topic changes.
  • Explain research or practice outputs and skill objectives appropriate to your degree type. Prepare the report, presentation and any required evidence of preliminary work.
  • Submit and attend the proposal review through the designated process. Record comments, the decision and revision deadlines; respond item by item and complete any required further review.

What to submit

  • A proposal report, research plan and presentation.
  • A stage plan, budget, risks and applicable approval records.
  • The proposal decision, revision-response table and approved or revision-pending status.

How completion is checked

  • Questions, methods and evaluation align, and the plan fits the remaining program duration.
  • The formal process, responses and versions are traceable.
  • Research requiring approval has the appropriate permission before it begins.
If requirements are not met

If the proposal is not approved, revise according to feedback and follow repeat-review rules. For major topic or method changes, check whether a new proposal review is needed. Verbal supervisory agreement cannot replace formal procedures.

05

Continue after proposal review, with reflection at agreed intervals

Research, professional practice and records

Build reliable evidence sufficient for the thesis while completing applicable practice and process requirements.

Before you start

  • The research plan, permissions and resources are in place.
  • Experiment, practice or analysis tasks have identified versions and owners.

What to do

  • Organize experiments or practice around the questions. Save data provenance, processing steps, parameters, environments, random settings and raw logs, including failed runs.
  • Use baselines, controls, ablations or case validation, avoiding data leakage, changing evaluation definitions and selective reporting of favorable results.
  • Include evidence and blockers in weekly reports, and turn group-meeting decisions into action items. Check coursework and annual requirements each semester; professional master's students should also retain practice plans, process records and assessment evidence.
  • Back up regularly and test restoration. Before using new data, changing its purpose, changing collaborations or exceeding the budget, check permissions and change procedures.

What to submit

  • Versioned code/analysis outputs, data documentation, environments and original records.
  • Experimental results, failure analyses, weekly reports and meeting action items.
  • Applicable practice reports, annual materials and change records.

How completion is checked

  • Conclusions trace to actual execution or practice records. Preflight checks and offline recalculation are not presented as complete experiments.
  • Key results can be checked repeatedly, and risks and negative evidence are not hidden.
  • Coursework, practice and annual requirements are tracked alongside research progress.
If requirements are not met

If results are unstable, check data, configuration and statistical definitions first. If evidence repeatedly fails to support a hypothesis, narrow the claims or revise the plan, recording supervisory feedback and necessary approvals.

06

Follow the applicable rules for each; do not assume one replaces the other

Mid-program review and annual assessment

Review program requirements, research progress and completion prospects, and agree on practical adjustments promptly.

Before you start

  • You have the current mid-program and annual-assessment notices.
  • Research, coursework, practice and output statuses have actual supporting records.

What to do

  • Compare progress with the proposal plan, identifying completed, delayed and changed work. Provide core results, a prototype demonstration or practice outputs.
  • Check applicable credits, academic activities, professional practice, ethics and research-plan requirements, addressing missing items early.
  • Assess remaining questions, thesis structure, resources and the graduation window. Submit a realistic follow-up schedule rather than deferring all unfinished work to the end.
  • Attend the formal review and distinguish supervisory advice from the assessment decision. Turn each required revision into a task, owner, deadline and review evidence.

What to submit

  • A mid-program report, presentation and evidence index.
  • Applicable annual-assessment and practice materials.
  • The official decision, revision plan and updated timetable.

How completion is checked

  • Existing materials support the progress report, with key gaps and risks identified.
  • Coursework, practice and other required training components have not been overlooked.
  • Revision conditions, review dates and possible consequences are formally confirmed.
If requirements are not met

If unsuccessful or revisions are required, provide missing materials, change the plan or apply for an extension under institutional rules. Claim a pass only after the required review, and disclose issues affecting later eligibility.

07

Write throughout the research; complete substantive review before external submission

Thesis structure, draft and revision

Present your own research through a complete argument, with consistent thesis text, data, code and conclusions.

Before you start

  • The main thesis question and core evidence are clear.
  • You have the thesis standards, template and confidentiality requirements for your degree type.

What to do

  • Map chapters to research questions, stating each chapter's purpose, claims and evidence. Professional master's students should present practice and applied contributions under the applicable standards.
  • Draft core methods and results chapters first, then align the introduction, related work and conclusions. Explain how previously published collaborative work relates to your own contribution.
  • Check original data, units, statistical methods and execution versions for every figure and table. Examine gaps in reasoning, counterexamples and limitations.
  • Submit a complete, reviewable version and question list to your supervisor. Retain feedback and itemized revisions, then check abstracts, references, appendices and formatting consistency.

What to submit

  • A thesis outline, chapter-evidence mapping and complete first draft.
  • Figure/table provenance, references and a contribution statement.
  • Review comments, a revision-response table and candidate final version.

How completion is checked

  • The thesis makes a complete argument, without substituting plans or placeholder text for results.
  • The student can explain all core content, with clear citation and reuse boundaries.
  • Supervisory review, formatting and later submission have a feasible schedule.
If requirements are not met

Add validation or narrow arguments where core evidence is insufficient. Fix structural problems before polishing language. If time is insufficient, assess an extension through the formal process.

08

Understand at enrollment; confirm each item before the degree application

Research outputs and degree eligibility

Complete the final verification of the requirements first checked at entry and topic selection, using official sources and supporting records. Recheck them whenever rules or plans change; do not wait until thesis completion to discover a gap.

Before you start

  • Your admission cohort, degree type and discipline or professional category are clear.
  • You have the effective versions of the corresponding output and degree-application requirements.

What to do

  • Check coursework, practice, assessments, outputs and other application conditions item by item, recording policy sources, applicability and consultation outcomes.
  • If papers or other outputs are required, check output type, authorship and affiliation, timing definitions and recognition procedures. Do not assume professional-program or another student's standards apply to you.
  • Record actual states separately: submission, acceptance, online publication, formal publication, patent application/grant, software registration, awards and practice acceptance.
  • Collect official evidence and request verification from the designated office. Record missing requirements, remedies and expected completion dates; follow the prescribed arrangements if outputs are insufficient.

What to submit

  • An applicable-conditions checklist and policy sources.
  • An output register, contribution statements and official evidence.
  • Recognition or eligibility-check results and a plan for missing requirements.

How completion is checked

  • Each condition has an applicable source and current status; unverified items are explicitly marked.
  • Output evidence is verifiable, statuses are not exaggerated, and collaborative authorship and affiliations meet the rules.
  • Eligibility is confirmed through the institution's required review process.
If requirements are not met

If evidence is incomplete, obtain formal documents or consult the recognition office. Report unmet conditions honestly and adjust application timing under the rules. Do not promise acceptance, patent grants or special exemptions.

09

Follow the institution's screening and thesis-review submission windows

Formatting, similarity, error and AI-use checks

Complete the separate required checks and ensure reports match the thesis version actually intended for submission.

Before you start

  • The candidate final thesis, references, contribution statement and output evidence are complete.
  • You have verified the institutional names, scope, platforms, thresholds and repeat-check rules for each check.
  • You have checked whether a pre-defense is required and its place in the process. If it is a prerequisite to thesis review, complete it and the required revisions before submission.

What to do

  • Check academic integrity, citations, data authenticity, authorship and confidentiality first, then formatting, attachments and thesis details.
  • Use the official similarity-screening process, checking matched sources and citations individually. Do not evade screening through cosmetic paraphrasing or character manipulation.
  • Some Chinese institutions call checks for typographical, formatting, figure/table, numbering, reference-format and layout errors non-academic-error checks (查非). Record each issue's location, correction and verification. Confirm whether your institution requires this as a formal stage and which scope and standards apply; it is distinct from similarity screening and AI detection.
  • Disclose AI tools, purposes, scope and human verification as required by the institution and output venue. If AI-detection results are required, retain them separately from the use statement and similarity report.

What to submit

  • A compliance self-check, official screening version and file-verification record.
  • The similarity report and applicable non-academic-error or other checking materials.
  • An AI-use statement, correction records and repeat-check results.

How completion is checked

  • Each report identifies a specific file version, checking time and official platform.
  • Each required check independently meets institutional conditions; passing one does not establish that all have passed.
  • Screening scores are indicators or procedural evidence. Alone, they neither prove the absence of misconduct nor replace human review.
If requirements are not met

If requirements are unmet, investigate, revise, repeat checks or request a review as prescribed. After substantive changes, check whether rescreening is required; do not reuse a report for a different version.

10

Within the institution's designated review window

Anonymous thesis review and responses

Submit the correct version under the anonymity rules, then respond and complete next steps according to the formal review outcome.

Before you start

  • Review eligibility and prerequisite checks are confirmed.
  • You have the anonymity rules, submission checklist, review procedures and appeal rules, including any applicable external-review arrangements.

What to do

  • Generate a complete archival copy and an anonymous review copy from the same thesis baseline. Handle names, affiliations, acknowledgements, output information, file metadata and identifying links according to the rules.
  • Check that references, attachments and necessary research information remain complete after anonymization. Do not remove or alter substantive content affecting review unless the rules permit it.
  • Submit through the designated institutional system, checking file previews, versions, details and receipts. Preserve reviewer independence; do not privately seek or contact reviewers.
  • Respond to every comment, revise and retain evidence. For factual errors or disputed findings, use the formal review or appeal channel within the prescribed deadline.

What to submit

  • Complete archival and anonymous review copies from the same source, with an anonymity checklist.
  • The formal submission receipt, reviewer comments and official outcome.
  • Itemized responses, revised thesis and applicable further-review/appeal materials.

How completion is checked

  • Anonymity and accuracy requirements are both met, and the submitted version is traceable.
  • Every comment has a clear disposition, with reasons and evidence for suggestions not adopted.
  • The need for another review or further assessment, and eligibility for defense, follow the official institutional decision.
If requirements are not met

If the review does not permit defense, revise, seek further review or defer as required. Do not declare a pass before an appeal decision, or influence assessment through informal contact.

11

After formal defense eligibility, following departmental notices

Defense application and formal defense

Explain the contribution, evidence and limitations through the prescribed process and undergo formal academic assessment.

Before you start

  • You have a clear review outcome and defense-eligibility confirmation.
  • The defense application, committee arrangements and public/confidential status are confirmed through the prescribed procedure.

What to do

  • Complete the application and submission checklist, checking versions of the thesis, reviewer responses and eligibility documents. If institutional rules place a pre-defense after review, complete it and its revisions first.
  • Present the question, method, evidence and contribution, clearly stating your own work and limitations. Rehearse with timing and prepare a verifiable demonstration.
  • Prepare responses to reviewer comments and check the venue, equipment, online arrangements and confidentiality conditions. Do not independently publish restricted theses or meeting links.
  • Answer honestly at the formal defense. Record questions, revision requests and the decision completely, and confirm subsequent deadlines and responsible people.

What to submit

  • The defense application and eligibility documents, presentation and rehearsal record.
  • Pre-defense comments and revision outcomes where applicable.
  • The formal defense record, decision and follow-up revision list.

How completion is checked

  • All prerequisites and defense procedures meet institutional requirements.
  • The presentation, thesis, output evidence and actual research are consistent.
  • Use the official wording for the defense outcome. Passing the defense does not mean the degree has been conferred.
If requirements are not met

If unsuccessful or major revisions are required, follow institutional rules for revision, reapplication and waiting periods. Do not promise the outcome of another defense.

12

After the defense, before the deadline for the current application round

Final revisions and degree procedures

Apply defense feedback to the final thesis and complete the required institutional review and submission.

Before you start

  • You have the formal defense decision, revision comments and submission deadlines.
  • The revision reviewer and degree-material submission channels are identified.

What to do

  • Address each defense comment and give revision locations. Explain any suggestion not adopted, and obtain supervisory or designated-person review as required.
  • Check the final title, authorship, abstracts, figures, tables, appendices and references. Confirm whether substantive revisions require additional screening or review.
  • Complete the degree application, library deposit, paper archive and required signatures. Ensure consistent versions across channels and retain receipts.
  • Check official graduation and degree-review statuses, recording defense approval, material acceptance, graduation review and degree conferral separately.

What to submit

  • The final thesis, itemized revision explanation and review confirmation.
  • Degree-application and applicable archival submission materials and receipts.
  • A formal status table and outstanding items.

How completion is checked

  • Every requested revision has a recorded outcome, and the final thesis matches the reviewed files.
  • Signatures, uploads and acceptance records are complete; outstanding tasks have owners and deadlines.
  • Degree status follows the institution's official decision, not an inference from a defense or draft system entry.
If requirements are not met

If the thesis or paperwork is returned, correct the specified issues and obtain renewed receipt. If a deadline is missed, contact the responsible office promptly and use the formal procedure.

Maintain throughout the research; complete acceptance before departure

Research archive, handover and departure

Keep research verifiable, restorable and reusable after someone leaves, with clear custody and access responsibilities.

Before you start

  • The final thesis, research outputs and official materials are organized.
  • The supervisor, project owner or designated recipient has defined archive scope and access permissions.

What to do

  • Create an index covering thesis versions, raw data, processing scripts, code, environments, configurations, logs, figure/table provenance, licenses and formal records. Distinguish originals from derived files.
  • Freeze final versions and generate verification records. Back up and test restoration under agreed rules. If data cannot be transferred in the package, record its storage location, access-request route and custodian.
  • Have the recipient sample key results and run instructions. Record unfinished experiments, known defects, collaborations, future output revisions and follow-up owners.
  • Complete equipment, funding, account and permission handover. Check retention periods, confidentiality and publication conditions. Personal portfolios and use after departure remain subject to permission.

What to submit

  • An archive directory, final snapshot, checksum and restoration records.
  • Data/code documentation, result index and permission register.
  • Handover acknowledgment, outstanding tasks and access-handling records.

How completion is checked

  • The recipient can locate and open core materials and verify key evidence.
  • Passwords are not stored in ordinary documents; sensitive material remains in authorized storage, and public versions are de-identified.
  • Responsibilities, retention periods and post-departure contact and usage boundaries are clear.
If requirements are not met

If materials are missing or checks fail, repair the archive and repeat acceptance. Explain the cause and impact of irrecoverable material. Empty directories or directory screenshots do not constitute handover.
