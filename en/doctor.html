How to use this pathway

This is a framework for research and development, not a substitute for institutional procedures. Verify whether a qualifying assessment is required, its order relative to the proposal, annual and interim assessments, the timing of any preliminary defence, recognised outputs and degree application conditions. Annual reflection continues throughout the programme. Academic and professional doctorates may recognise different contributions; this page does not prescribe a common programme length, publication count, publication category or screening threshold.

Suggested timing helps you plan; official deadlines come from the notices applicable to you. Complete the institutional-requirements check, then agree on submission and feedback dates for each stage.

LEARN / PRACTISE / REFLECT

Learning and growth

What you will learn

  • Develop a sustained research agenda around a valuable unresolved problem, establish the original contribution and its relationship to existing knowledge, and actively examine evidence that could overturn the claims.
  • Choose, revise and combine methods under uncertainty so that multiple studies support a coherent agenda, explaining the boundaries of theoretical, empirical or practice-based contributions.
  • Exercise independent judgment in research, academic exchange and collaboration, respond substantively to criticism using evidence, and preserve work that others can check and continue.

Check your starting point

  • Use your own training plan to list course, qualification, proposal, annual or midterm, achievement and degree requirements, preserving the applicable documents. Confirm the supervisory structure, decision responsibilities and review arrangements.
  • Map the argument of a completed study from questions to claims, key evidence and limitations. Distinguish your own contribution from collaborative work and identify an unresolved problem.
  • For the proposed research agenda, compare the closest work and present preliminary evidence or explicitly untested hypotheses, critical resources and failure conditions. Use a small investigation to probe the weakest premise.

Your responsibility

Maintain the research agenda, contribution boundaries and evidence gaps, propose priorities and methodological choices, and arrange necessary checks and scholarly discussion. If a key claim is contradicted, collaboration or data conditions change, or the agenda becomes infeasible, promptly present a diagnosis and alternatives to the supervisory team.

Guidance and support

The supervisor or supervisory team established by the program provides sustained challenge and support on originality, methodological quality, scope and development, with clear feedback responsibilities. They help the student engage with different perspectives and obtain necessary resources. Formal assessments and degree evaluations follow institutional procedures; group agreement does not replace independent review.

Feedback and revision

At reviews, examine whether new evidence changes the claims, how the studies jointly support the agenda, and whether critical objections have received substantive answers. Explain which suggestions will be adopted or deferred and why, then obtain appropriate review after adding evidence or revising the work. Recurring gaps should trigger changes to the agenda or methods rather than merely more experiments.

Evidence for the next step

At completion, hand over the central argument, checkable materials, open questions and continuing responsibilities. In independent research or professional work, be able to organize evidence, collaboration and quality control for a new problem. Record publication counts, publication status, defense outcomes and formal degree conferral separately; no single count substitutes for evaluation of research independence or original contribution.

01

Establish on enrolment and when supervision is arranged; review each semester and whenever requirements change

Programme Requirements, Supervision and Development Plan

Understand the research capabilities, formal conditions and support available, so that you can organise a sustained research programme and seek help when needed.

Before you start

  • Your enrolment, degree type, broad research area and supervisor or supervisory team are confirmed.
  • You can access the programme regulations, degree requirements and departmental notices applicable to your cohort.

What to do

  • Register requirements for courses, qualifying assessments, the proposal, annual and interim reviews, teaching or practice, the dissertation and the degree. Record the official source, prerequisites, submission windows and responsible office; list unresolved points for enquiry.
  • Assess your theoretical, methodological, technical, academic communication and research integrity skills using existing work or short practical tasks. Specify the capabilities to develop and the work that will demonstrate progress.
  • Agree with your supervisor or team how research discussions, feedback, disagreements and progress reviews will be handled. Distinguish routine advice, supervisory approval and a formal assessment decision.
  • Set up research folders, a reference library, version records, backups and a permissions register, and complete applicable ethics, safety and integrity training. International students should separately verify the languages of teaching, the dissertation, abstracts and defence, as well as resource access.
  • Prepare a semester plan with evidence, a feedback contact and a review date for each task. Address health, resource or supervision difficulties through suitable support channels without disclosing sensitive details in public records.

What to submit

  • A register of personal programme requirements, confirmed and unresolved items, and official sources.
  • A skills baseline and semester development plan with concrete work, review methods and priorities.
  • A supervision and communication agreement, research file index and backup recovery entry point.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain the prerequisites and responsible people for formal milestones; the appropriate programme office has clarified uncertain regulatory points.
  • Your supervisor or team has reviewed the direction, skills gaps and working arrangements; any required formal study plan has been approved through the institutional process.
  • You can locate your materials and support contacts, and have actually restored a non-sensitive sample from a backup.
If requirements are not met

Ask the responsible office to clarify uncertain rules and retain a pending status until confirmed. If supervision, language support or skills are insufficient, revise the plan with your supervisor and relevant support service before committing to a completion date.

02

Build an initial foundation early in the programme and extend it as the research questions develop

Foundations, Research Methods and a Map of Questions

Explain the field's key concepts, methodological assumptions and evidence limits, and judge independently whether a question merits investigation rather than merely repeat published conclusions.

Before you start

  • You have a skills baseline and study plan, with access to necessary courses, literature and computing resources.
  • You have an initial research area that can change in response to reading and investigation.

What to do

  • Identify the theoretical and methodological prerequisites for candidate directions. Demonstrate understanding through derivations, implementations, worked explanations or case analyses; reading counts alone do not establish understanding.
  • Keep search boundaries and selection criteria. Compare representative studies, conflicting findings and debates, mapping relationships between questions, assumptions, methods, data and evaluation.
  • Choose a baseline reproduction, theoretical analysis or field observation that can resolve an important uncertainty. Record actual conditions and failures, distinguishing published reports, your own execution and constructed examples.
  • Propose candidate questions and state the practical or theoretical basis, unresolved conditions, alternative explanations and observations that could overturn your current interpretation.
  • Explain the central argument to your supervisor or suitable peers and respond to challenges. Identify where you still depend on help and turn these gaps into the next learning tasks.

What to submit

  • A knowledge and methods skills checklist with derivations, implementations or analyses and remaining gaps.
  • A traceable literature matrix, record of disagreements and map of research questions.
  • Initial validation records and a comparison of candidate questions, including negative evidence and resource limits.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain central assumptions, methodological choices and failure conditions without relying on the source text, and distinguish observations, inferences and unknowns.
  • Your supervisor or invited specialists review literature coverage, foundational understanding and question selection. Peer comments remain feedback rather than formal assessment decisions.
  • Candidate questions have traceable support, and you can explain why existing approaches do not adequately address them under the stated conditions.
If requirements are not met

If you cannot explain a foundation or complete a minimal investigation, narrow the task, study the missing concepts and demonstrate it again. If prior work already answers the question or the claimed need lacks support, revise the question map and reconsider the direction.

03

At the formal qualifying assessment window; where no such assessment exists, use a research readiness discussion only

Qualifying Assessment and Research Readiness, Where Applicable

Explain disciplinary foundations independently, analyse open problems and answer follow-up questions, establishing the capabilities and formal eligibility needed for the next research stage.

Before you start

  • You have verified whether a qualifying assessment applies, its eligibility conditions, format, scope and order relative to the proposal.
  • You have foundational work, draft research questions and a skills gap assessment, and meet any applicable registration conditions.

What to do

  • Prepare the application and study checklist using the official assessment instructions. Confirm language, timing, submission method and permitted assistance; another student's experience is not a substitute for the rules.
  • Test understanding through derivations, oral explanations, method comparisons or analyses of open problems. State essential assumptions, counterexamples, limitations and uncertainties.
  • Practise questioning: answer independently first, then address gaps using feedback from your supervisor or peers. Record actual weaknesses without treating practice results as a formal pass.
  • Attend the formal assessment and obtain the result and any required corrective work from the department or assessment body. If no qualifying assessment applies, keep a research readiness discussion record without inventing a qualification decision.

What to submit

  • A record of assessment applicability and eligibility checks.
  • Preparation work, question-and-answer records and a skills improvement plan.
  • The formal result and follow-up requirements, or a research readiness discussion record where the assessment does not apply.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain key concepts and their conditions of use, and propose a reasoned approach to unfamiliar questions while acknowledging uncertainty.
  • Your supervisor reviews preparation and skills gaps; a qualifying assessment is passed only when the institution's designated assessment body records that result.
  • Resits, corrective work or non-applicability are accurately recorded, and prerequisites for the next formal stage have been checked.
If requirements are not met

Address specific skills gaps through further study and review. Following a formal unsuccessful result, use the required remediation, reassessment or other institutional process rather than bypassing a prerequisite.

04

Within the institutional proposal window, once the necessary preliminary evidence is available

Research Direction, Linked Studies and Formal Proposal

Independently propose a testable central question with contribution potential, explaining how individual studies connect and how the research plan will progressively focus.

Before you start

  • You meet the formal proposal conditions, understand the closest related work and have completed necessary preliminary validation.
  • There is verifiable evidence that the essential data, equipment, methodological skills and collaboration arrangements are feasible.

What to do

  • Express the central research question in a concise, discussable form, specifying its subject, conditions and value. Explain how the subsidiary questions jointly address it instead of merely listing technical components.
  • Compare intended theoretical, methodological, system or practice contributions with the closest work. For each claim, identify supporting evidence, competing explanations and results that could refute it.
  • Specify dependencies between studies, methods, comparators, evaluation criteria and data sources, together with ethics and permissions, cost limits, stopping conditions and alternatives.
  • Prepare the report and presentation in the official format, separating completed findings from planned work and using preliminary records to establish minimum feasibility.
  • Take part in the proposal assessment. Record each comment, actual revision, remaining disagreement and any need for reassessment. Keep supervisory advice separate from the formal decision.

What to submit

  • The proposal and a research direction diagram showing study dependencies and intended contributions.
  • A claim–evidence–alternative explanation table, preliminary validation materials and a research risk plan.
  • The formal proposal result, responses to feedback and tasks for the next stage.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain why the individual studies form one doctoral investigation and which findings would require a change in the claims or direction.
  • Your supervisor or team reviews feasibility and resource commitments; the institution's designated assessment body decides whether the proposal passes.
  • Assessment requirements have become concrete revisions or pending review tasks, with evidence, reviewers and the next checkpoint identified for each study.
If requirements are not met

If the direction is fragmented, contributions overlap or essential evidence is unavailable, reduce and restructure the subsidiary questions. After an unsuccessful assessment or a requirement to resubmit, address the formal feedback and confirm the required process before advancing the affected research.

05

Repeat throughout the research period after the proposal; necessary exploratory work may begin earlier

Independent Research, Evidence Testing and Iteration

Independently complete the cycle from a question to evidence, identify when conclusions hold or fail, and use these findings to revise the research direction.

Before you start

  • You have a clear subsidiary question, appropriate prior approvals and accessible research resources.
  • You can state the current claim, comparators, decision criteria and original evidence needed.

What to do

  • Create a task record for the current question, fixing the hypothesis, method, comparison conditions, analysis plan and decision criteria for this iteration. Label analyses added after exploration separately.
  • Carry out theoretical derivation, implementation, experiments or field research, preserving actual environments, original records, versions and failures. Separate premises from deductions and observations from interpretations.
  • Actively investigate alternative explanations and boundary conditions. Use counterexamples, ablations, robustness analyses, error checks or independent verification as appropriate instead of selecting only favourable cases.
  • Before supervision meetings, formulate your own conclusion, reasoning and next-step options. Record disagreements and unresolved questions, revise the plan based on evidence and follow the required process for major scope or resource changes.
  • Integrate each iteration into the research direction and dissertation draft, maintaining collaborators' tasks and contribution records. Retain unsuccessful methods and negative findings to support later decisions and avoid repeating failed attempts.

What to submit

  • Versioned research records, original evidence, code or derivations, and instructions for running or examining them.
  • A claim assessment table covering supporting and conflicting results, alternative explanations and untested conditions.
  • A research progress account, individual and collaborative contribution records, and the next iteration's task record.

How completion is checked

  • You can independently explain method choices, principal findings and failures, with claims proportionate to the evidence.
  • Your supervisor or team reviews the argument and research value. Suitable peers or designated reviewers check appropriate parts, with the actual scope of their verification recorded.
  • Key findings trace to materials and versions. Non-reproducible or access-restricted parts are identified, and replaying a saved record is not described as a new live execution.
If requirements are not met

If key findings are unstable, alternatives remain unresolved or resources are insufficient, identify the evidence gap and narrow the claim first. When support repeatedly fails, apply the stopping conditions, retain negative findings and propose a revised direction.

06

Maintain from enrolment and submit at institutional annual or interim milestones; research need not be complete

Ongoing Annual Reflection, Interim Assessment and Replanning

Use actual evidence to assess capability growth, research direction and programme progress, identifying unrealistic commitments early and making workable adjustments.

Before you start

  • You have the previous agreed study plan, research tasks and upcoming formal milestones.
  • You have assembled actual records of coursework, research, academic exchange and other applicable programme activities for this period.

What to do

  • Compare progress with the previous plan and formal requirements, recording completed, incomplete, changed and unknown items with links to work, original records or official results. Hours worked and publication counts do not replace evidence of capability.
  • Compare your current and previous ability to define questions, choose methods, analyse evidence and communicate research. Use concrete work to identify progress and remaining support needs.
  • Reconsider links between studies, conflicting evidence and major remaining risks. Explain the reasons and costs of continuing, narrowing, combining or stopping a line of work.
  • Submit your self-assessment to your supervisor or team and take part in applicable annual or interim assessments. Convert feedback into corrective tasks with owners, evidence, deadlines and reviewers.
  • Update the next-stage plan and expected route to completion. Verify and follow formal procedures for extensions, interruptions, supervision changes or major topic changes when required.

What to submit

  • An annual or stage self-assessment with programme status and evidence of capability development.
  • An updated research direction and risk assessment, formal assessment materials and actual results.
  • A corrective action tracker, next-stage plan and list of formal procedures still required.

How completion is checked

  • You explain differences from the plan and provide justified priorities and feasible next steps.
  • Your supervisor or team reviews the research and development plan; annual or interim assessment outcomes come from the institution's designated assessment body.
  • Closing a corrective task requires an actual review record. Resource and personal difficulties have appropriate support arrangements without unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.
If requirements are not met

Follow the required corrective work and reassessment process after an unsuccessful formal assessment. If research stalls or exceeds available resources, narrow the scope, revise the direction and arrange any necessary extension; do not rewrite the historical plan to conceal deviations.

07

Continue as individual studies yield verifiable findings; check again before the degree application

Original Contributions, Output Recognition and Scholarly Exchange

Explain the knowledge or capability your doctoral research adds, integrate individual studies into bounded contributions and accurately assess applicable output requirements.

Before you start

  • You have evidence supporting the central claims and records of conflicting findings.
  • You have the applicable output recognition requirements and can verify collaboration roles and permissions.

What to do

  • Connect each contribution to the central question, closest prior work, your own work, key evidence and scope. Check whether the contributions support one another and whether any claims overlap.
  • Distinguish theoretical, methodological, system or professional practice contributions from effort alone. Explain what understanding or capability has changed and what remains unresolved.
  • Check the recognised output forms and conditions for your degree type. Record work in progress, submitted, accepted, published or otherwise formally recognised as distinct states with supporting documents.
  • Confirm contribution records, authorship, intellectual property and material permissions with collaborators. Identify your individual work and required disclosures when using collaborative results in the dissertation.
  • Seek external criticism through submissions, presentations or suitable scholarly exchanges. Address substantive comments individually, adding evidence or narrowing claims where needed; acceptance does not remove the need for further scrutiny.

What to submit

  • A table connecting the central question, individual studies, original contributions and evidence.
  • An output register and requirements check with proof of actual status.
  • Collaboration contribution and permissions records, scholarly exchange materials and responses to feedback.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain originality through a complete argument rather than only a list of paper titles or performance gains.
  • Your supervisor or team reviews the contribution logic and boundaries of your work. The responsible office determines whether outputs meet application conditions under the applicable rules.
  • External feedback corresponds to actual revisions, additional evidence or reasoned explanations; unresolved disagreements and the true status of outputs remain visible.
If requirements are not met

If contributions are insufficient or disconnected, return to the research to establish the missing links or narrow the claims. Ask the responsible office to resolve recognition uncertainties. Following rejection, address substantive feedback rather than treating resubmission or an exaggerated status as a solution.

08

Write throughout the research and produce a complete reviewable version before formal submission

Dissertation Synthesis and Academic Integrity Checks

Produce a coherent doctoral dissertation with sufficient evidence and clear individual contributions, allowing readers to trace central claims and understand their limits.

Before you start

  • The research direction and central contributions are supported by evidence, with gaps and unfinished work identified.
  • You have verified the dissertation language, format, use of collaborative results and applicable integrity checks.

What to do

  • Map the argument across chapters, showing how the central question motivates each study and how findings answer it. Include a synthesis across studies rather than directly joining separate papers.
  • Link core claims to original evidence, derivations or material locations. Check notation, data, figures and consistency across versions, and incorporate conflicting results and limitations into the argument.
  • Clarify your own and collaborators' contributions, sources of published material, citations and necessary permissions. Verify cited originals and record actual AI uses, human checks and applicable disclosure.
  • Arrange successive reviews of structure, argument and expression with your supervisor or team, recording revisions and unresolved issues. If language assistance is needed, retain responsibility for the research judgments and content.
  • Separately verify academic integrity, any institution-specific non-academic issue screening or other special checks, similarity screening and anonymisation. Record each check's official basis, version, result and corrections; a detection score does not replace academic judgment.

What to submit

  • A complete dissertation draft, chapter argument map and evidence index for central claims.
  • Review and revision records, with collaborative contribution and material provenance statements.
  • Applicable integrity check records, an AI use record and the remaining submission checklist.

How completion is checked

  • You can explain the dissertation's unifying claim, chapter relationships and limitations, with traceable support for the principal conclusions.
  • Your supervisor or team has completed the agreed content review; designated institutional personnel or offices confirm formal checks and submission eligibility under the rules.
  • Language, citations, figures and contribution statements agree with the underlying materials. Each check refers to a specific version, and unresolved items are not represented as passed.
If requirements are not met

Return to research or narrow a conclusion if a core claim lacks evidence, and restructure chapters if the argument is fragmented. Correct integrity issues individually and obtain required rechecks; rewriting wording must not conceal provenance or material problems.

09

Follow the institutional sequence and submission windows; verify separately whether and when a preliminary defence applies

Preliminary Defence, Dissertation Review and Responses

Submit an accurate and complete account of the research, improve the argument through specific responses and substantive revisions, and maintain a clear formal review status.

Before you start

  • You have verified review eligibility, prerequisites, anonymisation scope, required materials and submission channels.
  • The dissertation has completed necessary internal reviews and integrity checks, and the intended submission version can be uniquely identified.

What to do

  • Check whether a preliminary defence applies, its position in the sequence, review application conditions and submission windows. Where required, present the research direction and contributions, then complete the requested revisions and confirmation.
  • Freeze the review version and record its date and checksum. Check anonymity in the text, supplements, file metadata and links as required, while preserving appropriate citations and complete evidence.
  • Submit through the formal channel and retain the receipt, version, material list and actual review status. Do not contact reviewers through informal channels to influence the assessment.
  • Analyse each comment as a factual issue, argument gap, methodological challenge or presentation concern. Provide revision locations, additional evidence or a reasoned explanation and identify unresolved points.
  • Use the prescribed channels for major revisions, further review or appeals. Ask your supervisor to check agreement between responses and the dissertation, then await the necessary formal decision before confirming eligibility for the next stage.

What to submit

  • Applicable preliminary defence records, a review eligibility checklist and frozen version records.
  • The submitted material list, receipt and formal review decision.
  • Point-by-point responses, the revised dissertation and further review or correction confirmation records.

How completion is checked

  • The submitted materials match the registered version, and anonymisation follows the requirements without undermining the argument.
  • You can explain how each substantive comment changed the research or argument. Your supervisor checks revisions and responses, while the designated review process determines the formal outcome.
  • Required further review, corrective work or appeals are recorded through the proper procedure, and the responsible office confirms eligibility for the formal defence.
If requirements are not met

Return to research or writing when feedback exposes a central flaw. After an unsuccessful review, follow the corrective work, reassessment or appeal process; do not bypass a formal decision by switching versions or treat supervisory agreement as a review pass.

10

After formal defence eligibility is confirmed; complete revisions and degree procedures by the applicable deadlines

Formal Defence, Revision Approval and Degree Application

Defend the research direction and original contributions clearly, acknowledge evidence limits honestly and complete the post-defence revisions and formal degree procedures.

Before you start

  • Defence eligibility, dissertation review and required corrective work have been confirmed through the institutional process.
  • You have the requirements for defence arrangements, materials, language, timing and the subsequent degree application.

What to do

  • Prepare a presentation around the central question, contributions, decisive evidence, your individual work and limitations. Ensure numbers, figures and claims match the current dissertation version.
  • Practise answering questions about alternative explanations, research choices and connections between studies. For demonstrations, prepare suitable offline material and state accurately what the demonstration covers.
  • Attend the formal defence and answer using actual evidence. Acknowledge uncertainty and record follow-up checks where needed; the authorised defence body produces the official record and decision.
  • Connect each defence comment to a revision and supporting evidence, obtaining approval from the designated reviewer before finalising the dissertation. Follow the formal arrangements if further research or another defence is required.
  • Submit the final dissertation and degree application materials as required. Track the defence outcome, acceptance of revisions, graduation review and degree award deliberation separately; passing the defence is not the same as being awarded the degree.

What to submit

  • The defence presentation, necessary demonstration materials and a record of their alignment with the current version.
  • The formal defence record, responses and revision approval records.
  • The final dissertation, degree application materials and a register of the actual status of each procedure.

How completion is checked

  • You can independently explain originality, methodological choices, evidence and limitations, with answers consistent with the dissertation materials.
  • The authorised defence body determines the defence outcome and the designated reviewer approves revisions. Supervisory guidance does not replace these formal decisions.
  • Degree and graduation statuses are supported by the relevant office records, with pending deliberations or missing materials explicitly identified.
If requirements are not met

If the defence or revisions are unsuccessful, follow the formal decision on further research, corrections or another defence. Complete missing documents and confirm acceptance; a successful upload alone does not establish approval.

Research Archive, Handover and Future Development

Make research evidence understandable and verifiable within its access permissions, transfer collaborative responsibilities and turn doctoral capabilities into a plan for the next stage.

Before you start

  • You have identified the materials to retain, submit and transfer, including their versions, permissions and recipients.
  • You have checked institutional archiving requirements, collaboration agreements and outstanding research or degree procedures.

What to do

  • Organise the final dissertation, original and processed data, code, environments, derivations, experiment logs, review records and permissions. Create a traceable inventory and retain necessary relationships between versions.
  • Arrange appropriate storage and access for restricted data, intellectual property and personal information. Review public materials separately; completing an archive does not confer permission to publish it.
  • Write recovery or verification instructions from the entry point to key results. Have the recipient or designated reviewer actually check a sample, recording the environment, successful scope, failures and parts that could not be verified.
  • Transfer projects, equipment, services, materials and outstanding work. Adjust access through the relevant management process without sharing personal passwords, and confirm responsibilities for pending submissions and future collaboration.
  • Reflect on your growth in defining questions, selecting methods, assessing evidence, communicating and collaborating. Prepare a shareable research account and next-stage learning plan, and confirm actual acceptance of institutional archives and subsequent paperwork.

What to submit

  • An archive inventory, version and checksum records, and access and retention instructions.
  • Recovery or verification guidance and the recipient's actual sample-check records.
  • Handover acceptance, an outstanding responsibility register and institutional archive receipts.
  • A review of research capabilities and a next-stage development plan.

How completion is checked

  • The recipient can locate materials, understand key dependencies and complete the agreed sample checks. Anything not actually verified is identified.
  • The supervisor or project lead confirms research handover, and the designated institutional office confirms formal archiving. Relevant owners have checked access, equipment and outstanding responsibilities.
  • You distinguish public from restricted materials, and degree, graduation and archive statuses all have actual supporting records.
If requirements are not met

Repair missing files, failed recovery steps or unclear permissions and repeat the relevant checks. Until the recipient confirms acceptance, retain the necessary access and responsibility arrangements; a compressed file or a shared link alone does not complete handover.
