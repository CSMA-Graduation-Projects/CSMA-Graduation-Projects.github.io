# AI assistance and disclosure record

Purpose: record AI tools actually used in reading, coding, experiments, data processing, writing or presentations to support human verification, disclosure and traceability. Whether use is permitted, how it must be declared and whether related checking is required depend on the applicable university, publication or competition rules.

Instructions: record uses that affect research content or outputs. AI output is not a source or an established fact. If no AI was used, say so. Record only verifiable tool and version information. If the service does not provide a complete version, write “Not provided” rather than guessing.

## 3. Human verification and evidence boundaries

- Literature and citations: individually verify [Existence, authors/year, original content and conclusion location]. Do not directly rely on a generated bibliography.
- Code and calculations: record [Actual execution, test inputs, raw logs and recomputation]. Mark generated code that was not executed as unrun.
- Data and images: record [Whether generated, changed or cleaned; method and original version]. Synthetic samples must not be presented as real observations.
- Text and argument: check [Facts, contributions, reasoning and limitations]. Language editing must not conceal substantive ghostwriting or unverified content.
- Evidence origin: distinguish [Live runs, saved replay, authors' results and constructed examples]. AI-predicted results are not experimental data.
