# Literature review and evidence matrix

Purpose: establish reviewable literature evidence for topic selection, proposal and thesis work, leading to comparison, limitations and research questions. The matrix is an analytical tool; the number of papers is not a substitute for review quality.

Instructions: define search boundaries before screening, reading and synthesizing. Preserve search dates and versions; record additions as databases change. Mark papers not read in full as “Abstract only” and do not use them to assert method details or experimental conclusions. Follow formal university requirements for citation style and review length.

## 3. Synthesis

1. Classification: [Organize by problem, method, assumptions or evidence; explain the basis].
2. Representative advances: [Findings supported by multiple studies; link reference IDs for each].
3. Disagreements and counterexamples: [Inconsistent findings and possible differences in tasks, data or evaluation].
4. Problems already addressed: [What existing methods achieve; avoid claiming a gap that has already been addressed].
5. Gaps requiring validation: [Evidence of the gap, real impact and missing validation; “Few studies exist” is insufficient].
6. Implications for this project: [Which question to retain, narrow or revise; what to reproduce].

## 4. Self-check and acceptance

- [ ] Citations resolve to official sources; versions, preprints and publication status are clear.
- [ ] Core claims have evidence locations; authors' conclusions are separate from personal inferences.
- [ ] Important counterexamples and related methods are covered, rather than only supportive papers.
- [ ] The review compares categories and narrows questions instead of concatenating summaries.
- [ ] AI-assisted searching or organization is recorded; references and content have been checked by a person.
