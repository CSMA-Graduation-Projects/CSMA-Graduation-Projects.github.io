## Undergraduate research training

Start with manageable tasks and learn to ask questions, test ideas and communicate evidence.

Undergraduates taking part in research training, undergraduate innovation programs or research-group projects. Choose a starting point that fits your preparation and available time.; Progress according to your skills and project needs, with agreed work cycles and review dates.

Research training prioritizes skill development and verifiable evidence. It does not guarantee publications, awards or admission to further study. Before reusing results in a capstone or thesis, check institutional requirements, your contribution and the permitted scope of reuse.

**What you will learn**

- Explain a bounded task in terms of its inputs, actions and observable results, using an actual example to demonstrate understanding.
- Complete a reading, reproduction or module task independently, preserving sources, versions, commands, successes and failures so another person can check the work.
- Use evidence to explain results and limitations, turn feedback into a manageable next task, and gradually rely less on step-by-step instructions.

**Check your starting point**

- Bring a course assignment or project you completed and explain its inputs, outputs, key implementation and one limitation. If you do not yet have relevant work, identify the missing foundations and agree on an introductory exercise with your mentor.
- Run a small public example that you are permitted to use on an available device, saving the environment, commands and actual output. If it fails, provide the full error and attempted fixes rather than claiming proficiency.
- Read a source directly relevant to the proposed task and cite the passages supporting your account of its problem, method and limitations. Also provide an availability plan that includes classes and examinations.

**Your responsibility**

Identify the point you understand least, offer your own explanation or attempt, complete the agreed minimum task and submit evidence. Ask for help with your records when you still cannot explain a key step at the agreed checkpoint, or when data access, equipment or course commitments block progress; do not wait to be prompted about every item.

**Guidance and support**

The mentor chooses a suitable difficulty based on demonstrated foundations, initially models how to turn a question into checkable steps, and explains the expected quality and review date. An experienced member may help troubleshoot the environment. The agreed mentor remains responsible for direction, scope and quality judgments; receiving help does not itself establish that the task has passed review.

**Feedback and revision**

At the first review, examine both the actual output and the student's explanation, identify one essential piece of missing evidence, and set a review date. After revision, try a related task with different inputs and compare how independently the student can diagnose problems. If the example runs but the student cannot explain why, strengthen the foundations before expanding the task.

**Evidence for the next step**

Before taking on a more independent project, assemble a checkable task, an explained failure case and a next-step plan. When moving into a capstone or competition, carry forward skills, records and authorized materials, then confirm the new task specification or current competition rules, individual contribution and formal requirements. Training review does not replace those requirements.

### 01 Orientation and mutual fit

Suggested timing: Before formally accepting a task

Goal: Explain why you want to participate, how much time you can commit and what support you need, then agree on a suitable starting point.

**Prerequisites**

- You have read the group's public introduction and considered how research training fits around coursework.
- Use channels confirmed by the coordinator. Do not submit personal application materials to a public repository.

**Steps**

- Summarize relevant courses, assignments or projects and questions that interest you. State honestly which tools you have not yet learned.
- List your weekly availability, examinations and timetable conflicts. Agree with your supervisor on the trial task, feedback method and how arrangements can be adjusted.
- Confirm the task owner, storage location, available equipment and accounts. Understand the boundaries for citation, data licenses, confidentiality and AI use.
- Turn the discussion into an onboarding checklist, separating confirmed arrangements from matters still requiring verification.

**Deliverables**

- A summary of your background and interests, and an availability schedule.
- An onboarding checklist and trial-task agreement, including the owner and review date.

**Acceptance checks**

- The student can explain the trial task's purpose and completion conditions in their own words.
- The supervisor confirms that the difficulty and resource needs are manageable and that coursework conflicts have been addressed.
- Both parties know how to communicate about blockers, leave requests or withdrawal.

If requirements are not met: If preparation or time is insufficient, reduce the task or complete prerequisite coursework first. Record the discussion and agree on a review date. Registration or joining a group chat does not complete this stage.

Next step: After the coordinator confirms the trial task, begin tools and research-practice training.

### 02 Tools, literature and research practice

Suggested timing: Early in the trial period, adapted to your starting skills

Goal: Independently run a small example, record the process and correctly explain where materials and code came from.

**Prerequisites**

- Agree on a small exercise, an available environment and a review date. Existing work may demonstrate a skill if you can explain and rerun it; practise the gaps identified, rather than repeating mastered exercises or skipping checks because of year of study.
- You know which materials must remain in controlled storage.

**Steps**

- Set up the development environment and record the operating system, dependency versions and startup commands. Use a minimal example to check the installation.
- Practice Git commits, branches, diffs and recovery. Check for credentials, personal information and unrelated large files before committing.
- Find representative literature, distinguishing original papers, tutorials and secondary summaries. Produce a reading note covering the question, method, evidence and limitations.
- Complete the exercise and necessary tests. If using AI assistance, record its purpose and personally run the code, check sources and explain key implementation choices.

**Deliverables**

- A runnable exercise, environment instructions and actual test records.
- Reading notes and a source list.
- Commit history and explanations of fixes to identified problems.

**Acceptance checks**

- The exercise can be rerun from the instructions, without omitted failure information.
- The student can explain inputs, processing, outputs and any cited or reused elements.
- Installation, screenshots and AI output are not treated as evidence that the task has been verified.

If requirements are not met: If execution fails, reduce it to a minimal example and record the error. If key concepts cannot be explained, practice and review them before starting a complex project.

Next step: Once the foundational exercise passes review, take on a clearly bounded research task.

Related templates: Onboarding and stage plan (onboarding.md), Paper reading and reproduction decision (reading.md), Experiments, reproduction and run evidence (experiment.md), AI assistance and disclosure record (ai-use.md)

### 03 Reading, replication and small tasks

Suggested timing: Before formal project planning

Goal: Use a small amount of actual work to judge whether a candidate question is worth pursuing and whether you understand the task.

**Prerequisites**

- The foundational exercise runs, and the task owner has identified public materials or data that may lawfully be used.
- The task has defined inputs, outputs, budget and stopping conditions.

**Steps**

- Turn a broad direction into a specific question, such as which inputs cause a method to fail and how that failure can be observed.
- Read related work and perform a replication, error analysis, data-curation task or module fix. Record the original method and your changes.
- Specify metrics and comparisons in advance. Distinguish authors' reported values, local rerun results, offline analyses and unverified conjectures.
- Collect successful and failed examples. Explain what the evidence does and does not support, then propose next steps.

**Deliverables**

- A research task sheet and literature comparison table.
- Verifiable code, configuration, logs and examples.
- A short task report covering conclusions, limitations, unresolved questions and recommendations.

**Acceptance checks**

- Results trace to specific inputs, versions and execution records.
- The student can explain sources of differences without claiming general effectiveness from one or two examples.
- The supervisor confirms the value, scope and resources for further work.

If requirements are not met: If replication fails, check dependencies, data and evaluation definitions first. If the question lacks value, close it honestly or change topic, retaining failure records in the report.

Next step: Make an evidence-based decision to continue or adjust before setting the project scope.

Related templates: Research task card (research-task.md), Literature review and evidence matrix (literature.md), Experiments, reproduction and run evidence (experiment.md)

### 04 Project plan and task breakdown

Suggested timing: After the small task is accepted, before sustained work

Goal: Turn a research idea into a plan with owners, deadlines and individually verifiable tasks.

**Prerequisites**

- Preliminary feasibility evidence exists, and the project fits your current skills.
- If applying to a formal innovation program, you have obtained the current year's notice.

**Steps**

- Specify the real setting, target users or study objects, central question, exclusions and expected outputs.
- Divide the work into literature, data, implementation, validation and writing tasks. Give each an owner, dependencies, deadline and acceptance evidence.
- Check computing resources, equipment, funding and data licenses. For human-participant research or sensitive material, confirm required approvals before collection or use.
- Agree on reporting intervals, collaborative contributions, authorship discussions and change procedures. Submit formal applications through the designated system and retain receipts.

**Deliverables**

- A project description and milestone plan.
- A task list, resource budget and risk register.
- Application materials, approvals and submission receipts where applicable.

**Acceptance checks**

- Every milestone has an observable output, rather than only labels such as learning or optimization.
- Scope, resources, permissions and responsibilities support the plan.
- Team members understand their responsibilities, and formal application status is supported by records.

If requirements are not met: If resources or dependencies are missing, reduce the scope first. Do not describe an unsuccessful application as an approved project. Revise in response to feedback and clarify whether work will continue as a training exercise.

Next step: After the plan and necessary approvals are confirmed, proceed with research and implementation against the milestones.

Related templates: Research proposal and review preparation (proposal.md), Research task card (research-task.md), Topic, plan and resource change request (change-request.md)

### 05 Execution, weekly reports and reviews

Suggested timing: Throughout the project, at the agreed frequency

Goal: Deliver inspectable progress consistently so that blockers and risks are identified early.

**Prerequisites**

- Tasks, storage locations, owners and feedback intervals are confirmed.
- The current work cycle has prioritized tasks and acceptance conditions.

**Steps**

- Advance code, experiments or analysis through small commits, recording changes, execution conditions and actual results.
- Report completed work, evidence links, reasons for unfinished tasks, support needed and plans for the next cycle. Time online does not replace outputs.
- Prepare specific questions and attempted solutions before discussions. Afterwards, record decisions, owners and deadlines; add important verbal decisions to the agreed record.
- If data changes, resources are insufficient or failures recur, submit a change note and revise scope and timing. Do not delay silently or show only favorable results.

**Deliverables**

- Versioned outputs, configuration and experiment/test logs.
- Periodic reports, meeting action items and updated task status.
- Stage demonstrations or review records, and change requests where applicable.

**Acceptance checks**

- Reported progress matches actual files, commits and execution records.
- Blockers have assigned owners, and evidence shows how feedback was addressed.
- Stage outputs can be run or reviewed, and practical conflicts involving coursework, health or other needs have been reasonably accommodated.

If requirements are not met: If a milestone is missed, document the gap and cause, reduce the next cycle's tasks and agree on a review. If sustained participation is not possible, explicitly arrange a pause or handover.

Next step: Once core tasks meet the agreed conditions, move to independent checking and communicating the results.

Related templates: Progress report and next-stage plan (weekly.md), Meeting, discussion and decision record (meeting.md), Experiments, reproduction and run evidence (experiment.md), Topic, plan and resource change request (change-request.md)

### 06 Result verification and communication

Suggested timing: Before closure, publication submission, competition entry or public release

Goal: Align claims, code and explanations so that others can understand and inspect the work.

**Prerequisites**

- Core outputs are available and original records are traceable.
- The intended output is identified: a report, software, demonstration, paper or another form.

**Steps**

- Check the work from a clean environment or have another member follow the instructions. Review data processing, metric calculations, failure cases and limitations.
- Write around the question, method, evidence and limitations. Give sources, units and version information for figures and tables, and cite original materials.
- List individual contributions, reused work, collaborative support and AI assistance. Confirm authorship and licenses; do not present others' work as your own.
- Check formatting, intellectual property, visibility and submission conditions for the intended venue or competition. Submit after the relevant confirmation.

**Deliverables**

- A technical report or manuscript, verification records and reproduction instructions.
- Contribution and citation lists, permission records and public-release check results.
- Formal submission receipts and output-status records where applicable.

**Acceptance checks**

- Key conclusions have direct evidence, and limitations and negative results are reported accurately.
- An independent reader can locate the outputs and understand how to check them.
- Submission, acceptance, publication, rights granted and awards are recorded as distinct states.

If requirements are not met: If verification fails, locate the discrepancy, add evidence and revise the claims accordingly. Do not publish material with unclear permissions; obtain permission or replace it first.

Next step: After the outputs pass review, complete closure, archiving and handover.

### 07 Closure, archiving and next steps

Suggested timing: Before project completion, transition to a capstone or withdrawal

Goal: Preserve a complete record and usable outputs, clarifying completed work and remaining responsibilities.

**Prerequisites**

- Outputs and outstanding problems are organized, and formal project-closure requirements have been checked.
- A recipient or approved storage location is identified.

**Steps**

- Organize tasks, reports, code, data provenance, environments, execution records and permissions against a checklist. Create a version index and backup.
- Have the recipient inspect key files and actually restore or run them. Record missing items, fixes and access permissions.
- Complete project closure, return equipment and settle outstanding expenses. Transfer ownership and access for organizational resources through the platform's authorized process; do not transfer personal account passwords.
- Reflect on skills gained and gaps, then agree on further study or a capstone transition. Include only authorized work with explainable personal contributions in your portfolio.

**Deliverables**

- An archive index, final versions and backup-verification records.
- A handover checklist, outstanding tasks and their owners.
- A skills reflection and formal closure confirmation where applicable.

**Acceptance checks**

- The recipient can locate, open and verify core materials.
- Equipment, expenses, accounts and unfinished work all have handling records.
- Public presentations have not disclosed personal information or unpublished work without permission.

If requirements are not met: If records are missing or cannot be restored, repair the archive and repeat handover. State the cause and impact of irrecoverable material; do not mark an incomplete package as accepted.

Next step: Close the project before starting a new one. A transition to a capstone requires a fresh check of task and degree-stage requirements.

## Undergraduate capstone / bachelor's thesis

Connect the topic, implementation, thesis and defense with clear deliverables at every stage.

Undergraduates undertaking a capstone project or thesis. Select evidence appropriate to an engineering, experimental or theoretical topic.; Work backwards from the department's current annual schedule. Official dates, formats and eligibility follow the notice applicable to your program.

The capstone must demonstrate your own integrated learning. Before reusing research, competition or internship work, check topic suitability, individual contribution, permissions and institutional rules. All required capstone stages still apply.

**What you will learn**

- Translate the approved task into verifiable requirements or research questions, linking each to design, implementation and test evidence.
- Complete your own work within actual time and resource constraints, explain key design choices, and manage exceptions, defects and scope changes.
- Use a consistent thesis, demonstration and archive to explain your contribution, results and limitations, and answer challenges with evidence.

**Check your starting point**

- Bring the capstone notice and official templates that apply to you, together with planned dates. Mark which eligibility, milestone and submission requirements are confirmed and which need clarification.
- Choose one central objective of a proposed topic, write a checkable acceptance condition, and provide relevant course work, a prototype or a small feasibility result as evidence of your current foundations.
- Sketch the proposed solution and actually check access to data, dependencies, equipment and permissions. Record critical gaps, who will resolve them and when they will be checked.

**Your responsibility**

Maintain the link between approved objectives and actual evidence, schedule implementation, validation and writing, and submit reviewable drafts with specific questions. If a design cannot meet an acceptance condition, a core test fails or a formal deadline is at risk, propose an evidence-based adjustment and seek confirmation from the supervisor and relevant administrative staff.

**Guidance and support**

The supervisor helps assess workload, disciplinary depth and design feasibility, reviews key evidence before proposal review, midterm review and final submission, and identifies revision priorities. Collaborators may check work or offer technical advice, but individual work must remain identifiable. Formal eligibility, defense and archiving decisions follow institutional procedures.

**Feedback and revision**

At each stage, use the same core objectives to check available evidence and gaps. After a change, rerun affected tests or analyses and update the thesis and demonstration. Close a feedback item by recording its location, new version, verification result and reviewer, rather than writing only 'revised'.

**Evidence for the next step**

Before entering postgraduate study or a more complex project, retain evidence of a complete problem-solving process and assess whether you can identify an unresolved question, compare existing methods and design a suitable investigation. Completing an undergraduate engineering system does not by itself establish research novelty; begin the next step with evidence for the new question and an appropriate research design.

### 01 Requirements, topic and task brief

Suggested timing: During the department's topic-selection and task-allocation window

Goal: Secure a formally recognized topic with a manageable scope and accessible resources.

**Prerequisites**

- You have the current capstone notice, templates and system entry point for your program.
- You understand the applicable credit, eligibility and supervisory requirements.

**Steps**

- Create a schedule of topic selection, proposal review, mid-program review, thesis checks, defense and archiving windows, recording each notice's source.
- Compare candidate topics by problem, existing foundation, personal contribution, technical risk and workload. Test whether critical resources are available.
- Agree with your supervisor on required work, optional extensions and explicit exclusions. Define acceptance evidence for each core goal.
- Confirm the topic and task brief through the institutional process. Check the title, supervisor, student details and output requirements, and retain submission confirmation.

**Deliverables**

- An institutional-requirements checklist and timetable.
- A topic comparison, confirmed title and task brief.
- Core scope, acceptance conditions and a resource-risk list.

**Acceptance checks**

- The topic fits the program's educational requirements, and the student can explain their own responsibilities.
- The task brief matches the information in the official system.
- The scope is feasible within the actual deadline, with plans for critical dependencies.

If requirements are not met: Remove nonessential features if the topic is too broad, or change approach if resources are unavailable. Follow required procedures for a topic change so that the thesis and official registration remain consistent.

Next step: After the topic and task brief are confirmed, conduct focused research and prepare the proposal.

### 02 Literature review and proposal

Suggested timing: Before the formal proposal review, allowing time for revision

Goal: Explain why the work is needed, what existing work achieves, and how you will complete and validate your contribution.

**Prerequisites**

- The topic, task brief and scope are confirmed.
- You have the applicable proposal template and review arrangements.

**Steps**

- Search relevant literature and existing systems, recording search terms, sources and screening criteria. Compare approaches and limitations.
- Turn the background motivation into requirements or research questions. Distinguish existing technology, intended reuse and your planned contribution.
- Describe the technical approach, data sources, evaluation or testing methods, schedule and risk responses. Run small tests of critical technology first.
- Prepare the proposal and presentation, responding to each comment. After formal submission, preserve the version, feedback and approval or revision record.

**Deliverables**

- A literature comparison, reference list and critical-technology test records.
- A proposal report, presentation materials and stage plan.
- A comment-response table and the official proposal outcome.

**Acceptance checks**

- Literature claims come from actual reading, rather than citation counts alone.
- The question, approach and validation method align.
- The formal proposal outcome is verifiable, and required revisions have owners and deadlines.

If requirements are not met: If the proposal is not approved, revise the question or approach in response to feedback and follow the institution's repeat-review arrangements. Rewording alone does not make an infeasible plan workable.

Next step: After the formal proposal review and required revisions are complete, refine the requirements and design.

### 03 Requirements analysis and detailed design

Suggested timing: Before substantial implementation

Goal: Refine the approach into a structure that can be implemented, tested and explained.

**Prerequisites**

- The proposal is confirmed and outstanding issues have recorded resolutions.
- The implementation environment, data and main constraints are clear.

**Steps**

- For engineering topics, specify user scenarios, functions, failures and quality requirements. For research topics, define hypotheses, variables, baselines, metrics and experimental conditions.
- Diagram modules, data flows or the research process. Define interfaces, inputs and outputs, data dictionaries and key algorithms.
- Map core requirements to tests or validation methods, covering normal, boundary, erroneous and resource-limited conditions.
- Assess privacy, data licenses, security and maintainability. Test high-risk elements with small prototypes before confirming the design version.

**Deliverables**

- A requirements or research-question statement, scope and acceptance table.
- Overall and detailed designs, with interface or experimental-design documents.
- High-risk prototype results and a testing/validation plan.

**Acceptance checks**

- Choose one core requirement or research question and explain its design interface or experimental variables, the planned test and expected observations. The chain must show how the requirement can be checked.
- Data sources, error handling and critical dependencies are clear.
- The supervisor can assess workload and main risks from the design.

If requirements are not met: If the design cannot support acceptance conditions, clarify requirements again. If critical technology fails its test, change the approach and record the impact, completing formal change procedures if needed.

Next step: After the design baseline is confirmed, implement modules and prepare mid-program evidence.

Related templates: Requirements, detailed design and test report (engineering-report.md), Experiments, reproduction and run evidence (experiment.md), Topic, plan and resource change request (change-request.md)

### 04 Implementation and mid-program review

Suggested timing: From design confirmation to the department's mid-program review window

Goal: Produce demonstrable core progress and adjust the remaining plan using the actual gaps.

**Prerequisites**

- The design and current task list are clear, and version control is working.
- Mid-program review materials, timing and submission methods are confirmed.

**Steps**

- Implement and test modules incrementally, retaining commits, environment details and execution records. Demonstrate core user journeys regularly to reduce late integration risks.
- Continue drafting the requirements, design and methods chapters. Record decisions that depart from the original design and explain why.
- Compare progress with the task brief, marking work completed, in progress or not started. Support the status with code, tests or analysis results.
- Prepare the mid-program report and demonstration, explaining key problems, remaining work and plan adjustments. Turn review comments into action items.

**Deliverables**

- A stage version, run instructions and a core demonstration.
- A mid-program report, progress evidence and thesis outline/draft.
- Review comments, a revision table and subsequent milestones.

**Acceptance checks**

- Key workflows actually run, and screenshots and demonstrations match the current version.
- Review materials distinguish completed work from plans, with feasible arrangements for unfinished tasks.
- The official review outcome and follow-up requirements are recorded.

If requirements are not met: If the core user journey is incomplete, prioritize a minimal complete path and pause optional extensions. Where corrections or another review are required, submit evidence according to the official instructions.

Next step: After addressing mid-program issues, complete the system or research outputs and validate them comprehensively.

### 05 Testing, experiments and analysis

Suggested timing: After core implementation, before finalizing conclusions

Goal: Use sufficient, verifiable evidence to establish whether the task brief's goals were achieved.

**Prerequisites**

- The version under evaluation is identified, and the environment and data can be located.
- Test cases or experimental procedures have explicit expectations.

**Steps**

- Use the requirements mapping to execute functional, boundary, failure and applicable performance/security checks. Record actual results and defects.
- For research topics, check data splits, controls, metric implementations and repeated-run conditions. Prevent test information from influencing method tuning.
- Fix problems affecting core goals and run regression checks. For unresolved limitations, state their triggers and impact.
- Generate figures and tables from raw records. Explain results, failures and applicability, distinguishing self-testing, independent checks and unverified claims.

**Deliverables**

- Test/experiment plans, raw records and results summaries.
- Defect and fix lists, with regression results.
- A requirements-to-evidence mapping and results analysis.

**Acceptance checks**

- Every core goal has relevant evidence; successful interface screenshots alone do not leave requirements untested.
- Results correspond to identifiable versions, inputs and execution conditions.
- Conclusions remain within the tested scope, and a reviewer can follow the verification instructions.

If requirements are not met: Fix and rerun failed critical tests. Add experiments or narrow claims where evidence is insufficient. Do not remove failure records to declare completion.

Next step: Once core acceptance conditions are met, finish the thesis and prepare formal checks.

### 06 Thesis writing and compliance checks

Suggested timing: Write throughout the project; prepare a complete draft before checks

Goal: Accurately present your own work and meet applicable formatting and academic-practice requirements.

**Prerequisites**

- Main implementation or research results are complete, with the evidence needed for the argument.
- You have the department's latest thesis template and checking notice.

**Steps**

- Organize the thesis around the problem, related work, design/methods, implementation/validation and conclusions. Identify your own work and reused components.
- Check text, figures, tables, equations, references and attachments item by item. Every number must trace to an actual record, and you must review AI-assisted content personally.
- Complete supervisory review, formatting checks, similarity screening and other applicable checks as required. Record the checked version and official reports.
- Address feedback and verify revisions. Ensure consistency among the thesis title, system registration, submitted files and demonstration version.

**Deliverables**

- A complete thesis draft, reference materials and figure/table provenance records.
- A compliance self-check, applicable screening reports and AI-use statement.
- A revision-response table, proposed submission version and confirmation records.

**Acceptance checks**

- The student can explain the methods and results throughout the thesis, which reflects their actual work.
- Formatting and academic-practice checks follow institutional criteria, without self-invented pass thresholds.
- Similarity results, AI-detection results and judgments of academic integrity are not treated as the same conclusion.

If requirements are not met: Correct unsupported statements and faulty citations using original sources. If formal checking requirements are unmet, revise, repeat checks or adjust defense arrangements under the applicable rules.

Next step: After required reviews and checks are complete and defense eligibility is confirmed, prepare the formal defense.

### 07 Defense preparation and participation

Suggested timing: After eligibility is confirmed, following the defense notice

Goal: Explain the problem, your contribution, results and limitations within the time limit, and respond accurately to questions.

**Prerequisites**

- Defense eligibility, session, time and required materials are formally confirmed.
- The thesis, demonstration and presentation use consistent versions.

**Steps**

- Prepare a presentation within the allotted time, emphasizing your key work, validation results and limitations rather than letting background dominate.
- Prepare repeatable demonstration steps, sample inputs and an offline fallback. Clearly label backup videos or screenshots as prerecorded material.
- Rehearse with timing, check equipment, fonts and network dependencies, and practice explaining sources, design trade-offs and failures.
- Answer honestly during the defense and record uncertain points for verification. Keep a complete record of committee comments and the official outcome.

**Deliverables**

- Defense slides, a demonstration package and rehearsal checks.
- Committee questions and responses, and the formal defense result.
- A post-defense revision list and deadlines.

**Acceptance checks**

- The presentation, thesis and system behavior agree, and individual contributions are clear.
- Materials and on-site arrangements meet the official notice.
- The student understands follow-up requirements for a pass, conditional pass or other outcome.

If requirements are not met: If the demonstration fails, explain accurately and use clearly labeled backup material. If the formal defense is unsuccessful, revise and apply for subsequent arrangements under institutional rules.

Next step: Complete and confirm revisions according to the defense decision, then submit final materials.

### 08 Final revisions, archiving and handover

Suggested timing: After the defense, before the department's archiving deadline

Goal: Complete formal delivery with consistent thesis, outputs and process records that remain usable.

**Prerequisites**

- You have received the defense decision and revision requirements.
- The archive checklist, platform, naming conventions and signature requirements are confirmed.

**Steps**

- Address every comment and obtain confirmation from the appropriate person. Produce the final thesis and revision explanation, rather than revising only the slides.
- Package code, configuration, data you may retain, test records and run instructions. For data that cannot be included, explain how it may lawfully be obtained.
- Check consistency among the institutional system, printed materials and local final version. Retain upload confirmation or acknowledgment of receipt.
- Hand over equipment, accounts, materials and collaborative tasks. Before public presentation, recheck personal information, permissions and intellectual property.

**Deliverables**

- The final thesis, revision confirmation and formal submission receipt.
- An output archive, directory index and verification records.
- A handover checklist and authorized presentation materials.

**Acceptance checks**

- Every revision required by the defense has a recorded resolution.
- The archive can be opened and checked, and important files are backed up.
- Graduation and degree status follow official institutional decisions. A successful defense is not described as completed graduation or degree conferral.

If requirements are not met: If the wrong version was submitted, promptly contact the designated officer and use the correction procedure. Repair incomplete or nonworking archives, then obtain renewed acceptance.

Next step: After archive acceptance, follow up on formal graduation and degree procedures and retain an agreed contact route.

## Competitions and innovation practice

Verify the rules before forming a team and building the project; retain submission, result and reflection records.

Undergraduate and postgraduate students entering subject, innovation, entrepreneurship or technical competitions. ## Master's research and degree pathway

From topic selection to degree procedures, complete research, reviews and handover step by step.

Students in academic or professional master's programs, with different program lengths and study modes, must use their own applicable study plan. International students should also confirm which provisions apply to their admission cohort and enrollment category.; Plan backwards from your individual study plan and annual notices. Research and writing can run in parallel; formal stages follow institutional prerequisites.

Academic master's programs emphasize academic research capability. Professional master's programs also address professional practice and application. Specific outputs and standards depend on the discipline or professional degree category. This page does not set institutional output counts, publication levels, screening percentages or deadlines.

**What you will learn**

- Use evidence from real problems and related research to formulate a bounded, testable and feasible question, explaining its relationship to the closest work.
- Independently design and conduct research or practice-based validation suited to the question, checking controls, data quality and contrary evidence and explaining where the findings apply.
- Organize evidence into a coherent thesis, manage training requirements, feedback, achievement status and handover, and progressively develop independent, evidence-based judgment.

**Check your starting point**

- Provide your applicable training plan and a checklist of courses, practice, academic activities and degree conditions, with official sources, gaps and contacts for clarification. Check achievement requirements at entry or topic selection, rather than waiting until the thesis is complete.
- Compare a relevant set of closely related studies, citing original passages and distinguishing author-reported results, your own reproductions and unverified interpretations.
- Submit a baseline reproduction, engineering problem analysis or feasibility experiment with actual versions, data sources, results and failures. Use it to propose a plan for missing skills and a next review date.

**Your responsibility**

Lead the collection of topic evidence, research planning and scheduling. Before discussions, submit your judgment, alternatives and decisions that need input. Seek review when evidence repeatedly contradicts a hypothesis, research boundaries change, or resources or formal requirements threaten the plan; propose a narrower scope, additional evidence or a revised approach.

**Guidance and support**

The supervisor advises on research positioning, methods and quality, helps identify critical assumptions and risks, and agrees on feasible review arrangements. Professional-degree students also confirm practice supervision and institutionally recognized assessment requirements. Peer reproduction and group discussion contribute evidence; formal training and degree reviews follow institutional procedures.

**Feedback and revision**

At topic, proposal and midterm checkpoints, review whether the question, method and evidence remain aligned and whether the student can explain the choices made. Turn comments into revision and validation tasks. When new evidence changes the conclusion, revisit positioning or design rather than hide counterexamples to preserve the original plan. Research and writing develop together, and important results are checked again before final submission.

**Evidence for the next step**

For doctoral training, identify a valuable unresolved question emerging from the completed work, then develop the case for original contribution, longer-term feasibility and systematic evidence. For professional work, transfer the research methods to requirements, validation and communication under real constraints. Existing publications or practice outcomes are starting points, not automatic satisfaction of admission or training requirements at the next level.

## Doctoral Research and Degree Pathway

Define questions independently, establish contributions through verifiable research, and connect individual studies in a coherent dissertation.

Doctoral students across study modes, programme lengths and degree types, including international students. Use the programme regulations that apply to your own enrolment.; Plan around formal programme milestones. Foundational study, research, writing and annual reflection overlap; revisit the research direction as evidence develops.

This is a framework for research and development, not a substitute for institutional procedures. Verify whether a qualifying assessment is required, its order relative to the proposal, annual and interim assessments, the timing of any preliminary defence, recognised outputs and degree application conditions. Annual reflection continues throughout the programme. Academic and professional doctorates may recognise different contributions; this page does not prescribe a common programme length, publication count, publication category or screening threshold.

**What you will learn**

- Develop a sustained research agenda around a valuable unresolved problem, establish the original contribution and its relationship to existing knowledge, and actively examine evidence that could overturn the claims.
- Choose, revise and combine methods under uncertainty so that multiple studies support a coherent agenda, explaining the boundaries of theoretical, empirical or practice-based contributions.
- Exercise independent judgment in research, academic exchange and collaboration, respond substantively to criticism using evidence, and preserve work that others can check and continue.

**Check your starting point**

- Use your own training plan to list course, qualification, proposal, annual or midterm, achievement and degree requirements, preserving the applicable documents. Confirm the supervisory structure, decision responsibilities and review arrangements.
- Map the argument of a completed study from questions to claims, key evidence and limitations. Distinguish your own contribution from collaborative work and identify an unresolved problem.
- For the proposed research agenda, compare the closest work and present preliminary evidence or explicitly untested hypotheses, critical resources and failure conditions. Use a small investigation to probe the weakest premise.

**Your responsibility**

Maintain the research agenda, contribution boundaries and evidence gaps, propose priorities and methodological choices, and arrange necessary checks and scholarly discussion. If a key claim is contradicted, collaboration or data conditions change, or the agenda becomes infeasible, promptly present a diagnosis and alternatives to the supervisory team.

**Guidance and support**

The supervisor or supervisory team established by the program provides sustained challenge and support on originality, methodological quality, scope and development, with clear feedback responsibilities. They help the student engage with different perspectives and obtain necessary resources. Formal assessments and degree evaluations follow institutional procedures; group agreement does not replace independent review.

**Feedback and revision**

At reviews, examine whether new evidence changes the claims, how the studies jointly support the agenda, and whether critical objections have received substantive answers. Explain which suggestions will be adopted or deferred and why, then obtain appropriate review after adding evidence or revising the work. Recurring gaps should trigger changes to the agenda or methods rather than merely more experiments.

**Evidence for the next step**

At completion, hand over the central argument, checkable materials, open questions and continuing responsibilities. In independent research or professional work, be able to organize evidence, collaboration and quality control for a new problem. Record publication counts, publication status, defense outcomes and formal degree conferral separately; no single count substitutes for evaluation of research independence or original contribution.

### 05 Independent Research, Evidence Testing and Iteration

- Submit through the formal channel and retain the receipt, version, material list and actual review status. Do not contact reviewers through informal channels to influence the assessment.
- Analyse each comment as a factual issue, argument gap, methodological challenge or presentation concern. Provide revision locations, additional evidence or a reasoned explanation and identify unresolved points.
- Use the prescribed channels for major revisions, further review or appeals. Ask your supervisor to check agreement between responses and the dissertation, then await the necessary formal decision before confirming eligibility for the next stage.

**Deliverables**

- Applicable preliminary defence records, a review eligibility checklist and frozen version records.
- The submitted material list, receipt and formal review decision.
- Point-by-point responses, the revised dissertation and further review or correction confirmation records.

**Acceptance checks**

- The submitted materials match the registered version, and anonymisation follows the requirements without undermining the argument.
- You can explain how each substantive comment changed the research or argument. Your supervisor checks revisions and responses, while the designated review process determines the formal outcome.
- Required further review, corrective work or appeals are recorded through the proper procedure, and the responsible office confirms eligibility for the formal defence.

If requirements are not met: Return to research or writing when feedback exposes a central flaw. After an unsuccessful review, follow the corrective work, reassessment or appeal process; do not bypass a formal decision by switching versions or treat supervisory agreement as a review pass.

Next step: Apply for the formal defence once the necessary review and correction confirmations are available and all institutional prerequisites are satisfied.

### 10 Formal Defence, Revision Approval and Degree Application

Suggested timing: After formal defence eligibility is confirmed; complete revisions and degree procedures by the applicable deadlines

Goal: Defend the research direction and original contributions clearly, acknowledge evidence limits honestly and complete the post-defence revisions and formal degree procedures.

**Prerequisites**

- Defence eligibility, dissertation review and required corrective work have been confirmed through the institutional process.
- You have the requirements for defence arrangements, materials, language, timing and the subsequent degree application.

**Steps**

- Prepare a presentation around the central question, contributions, decisive evidence, your individual work and limitations. Ensure numbers, figures and claims match the current dissertation version.
- Practise answering questions about alternative explanations, research choices and connections between studies. For demonstrations, prepare suitable offline material and state accurately what the demonstration covers.
- Attend the formal defence and answer using actual evidence. Acknowledge uncertainty and record follow-up checks where needed; the authorised defence body produces the official record and decision.
- Connect each defence comment to a revision and supporting evidence, obtaining approval from the designated reviewer before finalising the dissertation. Follow the formal arrangements if further research or another defence is required.
- Submit the final dissertation and degree application materials as required. Track the defence outcome, acceptance of revisions, graduation review and degree award deliberation separately; passing the defence is not the same as being awarded the degree.

**Deliverables**

- The formal defence record, responses and revision approval records.
- The final dissertation, degree application materials and a register of the actual status of each procedure.

**Acceptance checks**

- You can independently explain originality, methodological choices, evidence and limitations, with answers consistent with the dissertation materials.
- The authorised defence body determines the defence outcome and the designated reviewer approves revisions. Supervisory guidance does not replace these formal decisions.
- Degree and graduation statuses are supported by the relevant office records, with pending deliberations or missing materials explicitly identified.

If requirements are not met: If the defence or revisions are unsuccessful, follow the formal decision on further research, corrections or another defence. Complete missing documents and confirm acceptance; a successful upload alone does not establish approval.

## Learning and Growth

To turn a training pathway into a personal learning plan, establish what you can do now, what you need to learn next and what evidence would show progress. This guide connects undergraduate research, the capstone project, master's study and doctoral research through a repeatable learning cycle, with increasing responsibility for judgment and organization.

Independent learning includes asking for help, checking feedback and revising your approach. Supervisors, peers and university support services play different roles; include the help you need in your plan. These are learning and mentoring suggestions. Confirm formal course, assessment, output and degree requirements against the programme that applies to you.

### Find your starting point through a small task

Choose a small task relevant to your current stage, using materials and resources you are allowed to access. Try the four activities below in a single record. Keep the actual outputs and note the help you received, then decide what to practise. Previous course results provide useful context; also check whether you can apply that knowledge to this task.

#### Explain the task and its rationale

Describe the problem, inputs, outputs, key concepts and one concrete example in your own words. Identify the original paper or official documentation supporting your account. Ask a peer to pose a “why” question and record where your explanation becomes uncertain.

**Keep evidence:** a short explanation, concept map or reading note with sources, plus unresolved questions.

#### Carry out a minimal exercise

Follow the source material to complete a small program, experiment, derivation or analysis. Record the environment, inputs, steps and actual result, then try a changed input. If you cannot finish, keep a minimal failing case and its error information.

**Keep evidence:** repeatable commands, materials and outputs, or a failure record identifying the precise obstacle.

#### Compare and interpret a difference

Compare two methods or settings. Identify what stays constant, what changes and how you evaluate the result. Check whether data, resources or operating conditions could explain the difference. With limited examples, describe only the observations made and list conclusions they cannot yet support.

**Keep evidence:** a comparison table, links to raw records, an interpretation and factors still to check.

#### Turn uncertainty into a useful question

State what you expected, what happened, what you tried and exactly what remains unclear. Identify the help needed and who could provide it, or propose a small check that could rule out one explanation.

**Keep evidence:** an answerable question, the evidence to inspect and a plan for seeking help or testing the idea.

For each activity, record “completed independently / completed with prompts / not yet completed / not applicable this cycle,” with evidence and a reason. Use these states to choose your next exercise; they may differ across tasks. After receiving help, try a changed task to see what you can now explain and carry out.

Degree level alone does not demonstrate capability. Agree with your supervisor which goals and support arrangements apply at the start of each stage.

#### A running example: investigating a project's test failures

**This is an illustrative task design, not a report of completed research or experimental findings.** A beginning undergraduate might run a public project's tests, explain one failure and preserve repeatable steps. A capstone student might implement a narrowly scoped failure-localization tool and evaluate typical, exceptional and boundary cases. A master's student might establish a specific localization problem, compare suitable methods fairly and analyze failure conditions. A doctoral student might organize connected studies to explain factors that limit localization, develop testable contributions and discuss applicability within the available evidence.

Progression depends on asking and answering more demanding questions. Project size, lines of code and the number of documents can help plan work; assess growth through the quality of explanations, validation and judgment as well.

### Connect each learning cycle to the previous one and the next

Select an observable capability goal from your current training stage and connect these six steps in one record. Reading and practice can alternate. When a check reveals a gap, revisit the relevant step and retain the version and reason for the change.

1. **Establish your starting point**Gather evidence from explaining, carrying out, comparing and questioning. Record independence, help received and current gaps.
2. **Choose a stage and capability goal**Open the relevant pathway. State what you intend to be able to do, the deliverable, how it will be checked and the resources and support needed.
3. **Study and practise**Read directly relevant primary material, complete a minimal exercise and apply it to the real task. Record important choices, errors and attempts.
4. **Record evidence and check your work**Apply the agreed criteria, verify sources and versions, distinguish observations from interpretations and untested judgments, and list gaps.
5. **Obtain feedback and make corrections**Ask specific questions and turn feedback into actions. Repeat the affected checks and record resolved items and remaining disagreements.
6. **Transfer learning and plan the next step**Try using what you learned with a changed input, method or setting. Compare the help needed, then choose the next stage, further practice or a smaller task.

**End the cycle with an evidence-supported decision:** “goal met,” “partly met; further practice needed” or “revise the goal and check again” are all useful outcomes. Record the decision, reasons, next task and review arrangements. Passing a formal training milestone is a separate matter confirmed by the authorized body.

### Choose an action that addresses the obstacle

Identify the main type of difficulty and prepare a small but sufficient set of evidence. While awaiting feedback, continue reading, organizing or checking work that does not depend on the decision. Obtain the appropriate confirmation before proceeding with unapproved data access, permissions or formal requirements.

#### A concept is unclear: use one example

**Signs:** you can repeat terminology but cannot explain its conditions or why a method works that way.

**Action:** return to the original definition, write an example and a counterexample, and identify the sentence or reasoning step where your understanding breaks down. Ask a supervisor, teaching assistant or knowledgeable peer to examine that point.

**Recheck:** explain a new example. If you still need prompts at every step, revisit the fundamentals and narrow the current task.

#### Implementation is blocked: keep a minimal failing case

**Signs:** code, an experiment or data processing does not produce the expected output, and the cause is unclear.

**Action:** fix the versions, input and steps, then reduce the problem to a reproducible failure. Record attempts and outcomes. Ask your supervisor about methods and the responsible service or resource owner about environments and permissions.

**Recheck:** rerun the original failure after the change and add a nearby case. Explain the fix and the situations you have not covered.

#### Evidence is insufficient: revisit the claim and design

**Signs:** results conflict with expectations, comparisons use different conditions, or available results do not support the proposed conclusion.

**Action:** list the claim, evidence, possible explanations and the next check most likely to distinguish them. Discuss these with your supervisor or a methods adviser. Narrow the claim, revise the design or stop an unproductive direction when warranted.

**Recheck:** evaluate the original judgment against new evidence, retain negative and uncertain findings, and explain how and why the conclusion changed.

#### A message you can adapt when asking for help

“I am working on [stage and task]. I expected [specific result], but observed [what happened and evidence link]. I have tried [actions and outcomes]. My remaining question is [one specific uncertainty]. Could [contact] help confirm [an interpretation, choice or resource]? After receiving feedback, I will recheck it using [method] and update the record by [a mutually agreed time].”

Agree on checkpoints and how to handle obstacles at the start. Report and adjust when repeated attempts no longer produce useful information or a blockage affects an agreed milestone; you can seek help before exhausting every possible attempt. Pause the relevant activity and contact the responsible person if research integrity, personal safety or data authorization is in question.

### Review output quality and capability growth separately

Use an existing weekly report, group meeting or stage review where possible to avoid duplicate paperwork. A short task may need a review at completion; ongoing research may benefit from agreed intervals and reviews triggered by important events. Set the frequency according to dependencies, feedback needs and personal circumstances. This guide establishes no universal duration or formal university threshold.

- **Start with an observable capability:** for example, “explain the assumptions of this method” or “reproduce the experiment and investigate differences.” State the starting evidence and the conditions for checking progress.
- **Connect learning to outputs:** record primary-source titles, relevant locations and their purpose. Turn reading into a derivation, implementation, comparison or question, with links to versions, data, logs and materials.
- **Assess output quality:** check correctness, completeness, repeatability, consistency between claims and evidence, and missing formal submission items. Retain failed and incomplete results accurately.
- **Assess capability growth:** compare with the starting point and explain what you can now explain, perform, compare or judge independently, and where prompts remain necessary. Identify your own contribution and the help received within team outputs.
- **Follow feedback through:** record each comment, the reason for acting on or deferring it, evidence of the change and the recheck result. For disagreements, identify further evidence needed and the next discussion.
- **Plan transfer and the next step:** try again after changing a meaningful condition. Use the result to move on, practise further or revise the goal, with resources, support contacts and review arrangements specified.

**Brief example entry (a plan awaiting execution):** capability: “explain the difference between a test failure and a code defect”; starting point: “I can currently repeat the error message but have not checked environmental causes”; learning task: “read the project's testing documentation and design checks that distinguish two possible causes”; planned evidence: “commands, environment details, outputs and an interpretation table”; transfer task: “apply the reasoning to a different failure.” Enter actual outcomes and feedback after doing the work; leave completion unconfirmed beforehand.

Language expression, software fluency and research judgment can develop at different rates. ### Connect daily work to the term's goals

At the start of a working day, choose the most important deliverable and check its inputs and resources. At the end, record completed work, file versions, experiment status and the next step. ### Make reports and meetings lead to action

For collaboration across languages, agree on meeting and feedback languages, share terminology and topics in advance, and confirm understanding with a short action record afterward. A progress report covers the original weekly plan, actual work and evidence, what was learned, unresolved questions and next week's deliverables. Separate facts from explanations: “two of three runs failed” is an observation; “insufficient resources may be responsible” is an explanation to test. A reading update should explain what you understood and how it relates to the current question. An implementation update should identify verified features and work still incomplete.

Send materials at the agreed time before the meeting and list specific questions. Prioritise the evidence behind conclusions, important difficulties and choices between approaches. Afterwards, record decisions, actions, owners, review dates and evidence to add. Mark unresolved matters as pending confirmation; do not record an assumption as a supervisor's decision. Begin the next discussion by reviewing previous actions before moving to new questions.

When submitting a draft to your supervisor, identify the version, main changes, requested scope of feedback and planned submission date, allowing reasonable review time. If feedback is later than agreed, send a brief reminder identifying the affected milestone. If an official deadline is at risk, also contact the responsible department staff to seek a workable arrangement.

### Distinguish leave, internships and required professional practice

Leave changes study or attendance arrangements for a specified period. An internship is a work or project experience within or outside the university. Professional practice is a component specified by a program's curriculum. An internship does not automatically fulfil that component, and an employer's certificate does not automatically replace university assessment. Before departure, check who must receive the application, the approval order, insurance or safety requirements, confidentiality and ownership of outputs, and effects on coursework and research milestones.

An application should give dates, arrangements, effects on tasks, continuity plans and necessary contact channels, and should be submitted through the designated controlled channel. Share sensitive health information only with authorised personnel. Before practical training, agree on learning goals, supervisors, tasks and assessment materials. Keep accurate records during the placement, submit the required report and evaluations afterwards, and mark the program component complete only after formal university recognition.

### Estimate resources before scheduling experiments

Before requesting a server, model API, dataset or conference funding, state the purpose, expected duration or request volume, spending limit, data permissions and how resources will be released afterwards. Start with a small validation run before scaling up. Record resource usage and costs; stop expanding the run and discuss the situation if spending departs from the budget. Book shared equipment as agreed, and leave the purpose and stopping conditions for long-running tasks so others know how to handle them.

Follow institutional procedures for expenditure and reimbursement and retain compliant receipts. Before a collaboration starts, agree on permitted data use, authorship and intellectual property. An internship or competition does not itself authorise taking an organisation's data, code or unpublished results outside that organisation.

### Support, wellbeing and corrections to records

If persistent obstacles, excessive workload or physical or mental health affect your studies, speak early with your supervisor, student adviser or university support service and agree on task adjustments and a review date. Questions about supervision, assessment or rights can be raised through the consultation, feedback or appeal channels published by your institution. Applicable rules determine the procedure. Evaluation should focus on work and evidence while protecting personal privacy.

AI may help summarise meetings and reports, provided the materials may be shared with the chosen tool. AI-generated conclusions about attendance or progress must not directly determine assessment. When correcting a record, retain the explanation, date and person confirming the correction so later readers know which version is valid.

## Academic practice and submission checks

Academic integrity applies throughout topic selection, reading, implementation, experimentation and writing. A thorough pre-submission check is necessary but cannot replace accurate working records. ### Check authenticity and provenance first

Every important thesis claim should be traceable to its supporting literature, data, run records or analysis. Distinguish results reported by the original authors, results you have actually reproduced and hypotheses still to be tested. When comparing with published results, identify their source and conditions. A successful demonstration, a completed run and sufficient support for a research conclusion represent different levels of evidence.

Experiment records should include at least the code version, environment, data version and split, configuration, execution command, time, exit status, logs and raw outputs. Retain failures, timeouts and anomalies, including or excluding them under rules explained in advance. Do not selectively retain favourable samples to improve a metric without reporting it. Generate figures and tables from saved data, retain processing scripts and check units, sample sizes, errors and captions.

For third-party code, models, images and data, check provenance, licences, use restrictions and citation requirements. Availability for download does not imply unrestricted permission to publish. For studies involving people, interviews, experiments or personal data, determine before recruitment or collection whether ethics review, informed consent or authorisation is required, and follow university procedures. A self-check completed after the research cannot stand in for prior approval.

### Understand and address each citation and similarity issue

Keep citation records while writing: where a claim comes from, its location in the source and how it supports your argument. Identify direct quotations, and organise paraphrases in your own words with accurate attribution. Read and verify the references you cite; avoid nonexistent titles, authors, years or DOIs. Changing a few words does not make another person's ideas, figures or code your own work.

1. **Confirm rules and versions.** Check the current official notice for the designated platform, material to be checked, submission contents, decision criteria and revision or retesting process. Do not invent an unconfirmed threshold.
2. **Check content and citations.** Match in-text citations with bibliography entries, identify sources for figures and tables, and explain contributions and reuse in collaborative work or your own earlier outputs.
3. **Use the designated channel.** Submit the complete draft reviewed by your supervisor and record its filename, version, date and checksum. Do not upload unpublished theses to unverified checking websites.
4. **Read the report item by item.** Locate matching passages and assess citation accuracy, problematic wording and whether standard definitions need explanation. Correct the underlying issue and retain the reason and location of each change.
5. **Follow the required review procedure.** Use formal channels for any retest or approval and retain reports and receipts. Passing a similarity check does not establish research quality, appropriate authorship or permitted data use.

**Correct the work rather than trying to evade detection.** Do not turn text into images, insert irrelevant text, substitute characters deliberately or fabricate citations to reduce a reported score. This page sets no universal similarity threshold and does not equate a score directly with a finding of misconduct.

### Check presentation and other non-academic errors

Some Chinese universities use the term “cha-fei” for checks of non-academic errors, including typos, formatting, figures and tables, numbering, bibliographic details and layout. This is distinct from a similarity check. Confirm whether your university requires a formal check, its order, sampling procedure and assessment criteria in the [local requirements record](https://csma-graduation-projects.github.io/en/policies.html#local-check) . The following methods can also be used for general writing-quality review.

Check each item against the final exported thesis

| Area | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Basic information and structure | Check required Chinese and English titles, degree type, abstracts, keywords, contents, chapters, declarations and appendices against the university's current template. |
| Text and terminology | Find typos, remaining placeholders, repeated paragraphs, inconsistent terms and abbreviations missing an explanation at first use. |
| Figures, tables and equations | Check legibility, captions, units, symbol definitions, numbering, in-text references and page breaks. Compare numerical values with the original results. |
| References and numbering | Check bibliography fields, citation order, page numbers, cross-references and automatic numbering. Resolve messages such as “Reference source not found.” |
| Pages and export | Check headers, footers, page numbers, pagination, blank pages, fonts, searchable text and bookmarks. Inspect the exported PDF from beginning to end. |

Record the page or paragraph, issue type, correction, review result and date. Export again after changes and inspect affected contents lists, numbering and page breaks; do not correct the source while submitting an old PDF. Before official submission, reopen the exact file to be uploaded rather than inferring its version from the filename.

### Explain, verify and take responsibility for AI use

First check the applicable rules of the university, course, competition and publication venue. Record the tool name and version, date, purpose, affected sections or code, and your verification method. Accurately distinguish language editing, code suggestions, information organisation and content generation; disclose use in the required place and format. An assistive tool cannot replace research and reasoning you are required to perform yourself.

Verify AI-provided references, facts, formulas, code and interpretations individually. Read and test code and check its licence; successful execution alone does not prove correctness. Do not present generated examples as real experiment records or user feedback. Enter unpublished papers, personal information, review comments or restricted data only when both the tool and the proposed use of those materials are authorised.

If the university requires AI-generated-content detection, record the policy version, interpretation of results and review route, then follow the applicable process. This page sets no AI-detection threshold, provides no evasion methods and does not automatically infer a violation from one score. If questions arise, retain writing versions, notes, experiments and revision history for review by authorised personnel.

### Review anonymity beyond the main text

Read the specific anonymity requirements before creating the review copy from the approved thesis version. Areas commonly requiring attention include the cover, acknowledgements, author and supervisor details, research outputs, self-citation wording, project information, headers and footers, appendices, text inside images, attachments and filenames. Anonymity does not mean removing necessary scholarly references. Handle identifying information under the university's rules while maintaining academic accuracy.

Check PDF or document properties for authors and affiliations, comments, tracked changes and hidden content. Also inspect supplementary material, demonstration videos, repository links, READMEs, commit history and access pages for identifying information. If reviewers need code, use an approved anonymisation method; do not destroy repository history or delete original materials to achieve anonymity. Ask the department how to provide materials if reviewability and anonymity requirements appear to conflict.

Retain the complete thesis, the review copy and a record linking them, along with file checksums and system submission receipts. If an important issue is found after the version is frozen, contact the designated staff and follow the withdrawal or correction procedure. Do not privately replace the file and still claim it is the same reviewed version.

### Make corrections traceable and reviewable

First locate the affected version and scope, then distinguish layout errors, insufficient evidence, inappropriate citation and potential integrity issues. Pause further submission of the affected material, preserve original records and consult your supervisor and the appropriate staff. For each comment, record your response, change location, evidence and unresolved matters. Explain why an unadopted suggestion was not followed; “revised” alone is not an adequate response.

Record group review, formal similarity and presentation checks, external review and defense outcomes separately. Suspected misconduct is determined and handled through university rules and formal procedures. For a disputed academic evaluation, consult the university's published review channels. Keep individual comments, detection reports and review materials access-controlled rather than displaying them on the public website.

### Create an index that makes materials easy to find

Follow the project's existing conventions. Do not force active repositories to be moved or renamed merely for a uniform appearance. If materials are held across several approved systems, use an archive index to state each location, access conditions, responsible person and version. A public index must not disclose controlled addresses, accounts or individual evaluations. Keep an index requiring permission within the appropriate controlled space.

A new project may adapt the structure below, combining or omitting parts that do not apply. Official documents required by the university remain in its designated systems. Clear organisation matters more than the number of folders.

```text
project/
├── README.md              # Contents, scope, entry points and run instructions
├── requirements/          # Requirements, scope, plans and changes
├── literature/            # Search records, reference index and reading notes
├── proposal/              # Research plan and applicable proposal-review materials
├── code/                  # Source, dependencies and run scripts
├── data/
│   ├── raw/               # Original data or approved acquisition instructions
│   └── derived/           # Cleaned and processed data
├── experiments/           # Configurations, run logs and environment records
├── results/               # Result tables, analysis and plotting scripts
├── thesis/                # Drafts, figure sources and submission versions
├── reviews/               # Access-controlled feedback and revision records
└── archive/               # Delivery inventory, checks and acceptance records
```

Use explicit dates and versions for milestone submissions rather than names such as “final2” or “last revision.” Keep separate thesis versions for external review, defense and the corrected final submission, marking their official submission status. Identify the corresponding code commit or release. Matching filenames do not establish matching content; retain checksums for important deliveries.

### Separate source material, working files and public outputs

Access levels and permitted uses

| Material category | Typical contents | How to handle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Controlled source material | Original collected data, authorisations, participant information and raw experimental outputs. | Preserve provenance and original form, with restricted access. Analyse copies or derived data without overwriting the originals. |
| Controlled working material | Individual plans, progress reports, unpublished papers, review comments, research records and internal handover records. | Grant access according to responsibility and identify the valid version and owner. Do not place it on a public website. |
| Approved public outputs | Papers, code, sample data, demonstrations, blank templates and documentation cleared for release. | Create a separate release package. Check licences, privacy, attribution and confidentiality restrictions before confirming publication. |

Even cleaned or anonymised data requires a check of re-identification risks, permission conditions and licences. Removing names alone does not authorise publication. For data that cannot be shared, consider providing a data dictionary, access application procedure, processing scripts and synthetic examples. State clearly that examples are demonstrations, not actual experimental results.

**A file hidden from a webpage is not necessarily private.** Files in a public GitHub repository may remain accessible through the repository and its history even if unlinked, collapsed or excluded from the website build. Store completed personal materials in an approved access-controlled space.

### Record essential information for each important item

Assign each item a stable identifier in the delivery inventory and record its name, purpose, format, version or date, owner, source, related task or thesis location, storage location, access level, licence conditions and checksum. Explain fields that do not apply; do not use an empty field to hide an unconfirmed status.

For data, also record collection or download dates, dataset version, field definitions, units, sample size, cleaning and exclusion rules, split method and generation scripts. For runs, record the code version, dependencies, configuration, data version, command, resources, start and end times, exit status and output location. Link figures and tables to input data and generation scripts, establishing a trace from thesis claim to figure or table, result, run, code and data.

For materials that licensing restrictions prevent you from including, document the restriction, an authorised acquisition route and the responsible contact role; do not supply personal account credentials. For materials from a partner organisation, confirm what the group may retain, reuse and publish. Record contributions and authorship agreements for jointly produced code and papers.

### Write a README the recipient can actually follow

1. **State the scope.** List the delivered contents, thesis or project version, verified results and known limitations, including features that need external accounts or restricted data.
2. **Describe the environment.** Provide the operating system, language and key dependency versions, hardware requirements, installation commands and environment files. Identify model or service versions and access conditions.
3. **Explain the data.** Give a lawful acquisition method, destination, preprocessing commands and data verification steps. Do not leave the recipient to guess the directory structure.
4. **Provide a minimal run.** Supply a verifiable starting command, expected output, estimated time and resource needs, followed by the entry point for the complete experiment.
5. **Explain the results.** Identify output files, metric calculations and plotting procedures, expected tolerances and sources of randomness. For results that cannot be repeated, state why and how they can be checked.

Before delivery, follow the README from a clean directory. If a complete rerun is genuinely infeasible, retain run logs, raw outputs, analysis scripts and an explanation of the limitations. Label a full rerun, recomputation from saved records and file-only inspection separately. A structural check does not establish successful reproduction.

Checksums help detect changes to files. Run the following example within the archive directory; the actual file list should match your delivery inventory:

```text
# macOS: create and verify a SHA-256 manifest
shasum -a 256 README.md thesis/final.pdf > SHA256SUMS
shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS

# Equivalent commands on Linux
sha256sum README.md thesis/final.pdf > SHA256SUMS
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
```

Regenerate the manifest after changing files. A successful checksum comparison shows consistency with the manifest, not that the content is correct or its provenance authentic. After a backup, restore selected important files and check that they open, can be read and have usable permissions. Successful synchronisation does not establish that recovery has been tested.

### Complete the handover through recipient acceptance

Minimum archive and handover acceptance checks

| Check | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Official documents | The final thesis, declarations, revision records and other university-required materials have been submitted as required, with the relevant receipts or status. |
| Completeness and consistency | Every inventory item is checked; the relationship between thesis, code, data and results is clear; important file checksums match. |

### Agree on retention and access after leaving the group

Set retention periods according to university rules, funding conditions, data licences, ethics requirements and collaboration agreements. Record the basis and the person responsible at expiry; do not assign every material a universal retention period. Expiry does not trigger automatic deletion. First check continuing retention duties, review needs and other restrictions, then follow an approved process and retain a record.

Transfer responsibilities through organisation ownership transfer, membership permission changes, service-account management and key rotation, without handing over personal passwords. Check the owners, billing and recovery arrangements for repositories, storage, servers, model services, domains and other resources. Confirm the new owner can access them before withdrawing permissions that are no longer needed. If collaboration continues, agree on the scope and duration of continued access.

- **Capstone project:** check topic approval, the task brief, proposal, progress reviews, thesis format, abstracts, assessment and defence languages in advance. - **Competitions:** before registering, ask the organiser about eligibility for international students and your year of study, team rules, submission languages, public presentation and use of outputs. Record the rules version, eligibility confirmation, responsibilities and submission receipt. A prize does not automatically replace graduation requirements.
- **Completion and next steps:** organise reproducible code or experimental materials, documentation and your reflection; distinguish public work from restricted materials. Record what remains for credit recognition, the capstone project or further research.

### Master's: align research progress with formal programme stages

Confirm whether you are pursuing an academic or professional degree, the applicable programme requirements and any practice component before making your timeline. The same research task may support different formal documents. Record supervisory feedback separately from institutional decisions.

- **Topic and literature review:** develop a topic statement covering the question, prior work, available data and feasible scope. Agree on languages for reading discussions, group meetings and written feedback; retain the bibliography, problem evidence and supervisor's comments.
- **Proposal and midterm review:** use the applicable documents to check prerequisites, formats, presentation languages, timing and responsible offices. Submit the report, evidence and plan, then retain official comments, responses and follow-up decisions. A group rehearsal is not a formal pass.
- **Research outputs and practice:** check required research achievements, practice and academic activities item by item. Record the applicable rule, recognition criteria, your contribution, available evidence and missing items. Do not borrow numerical requirements from another cohort or degree type.
- **Thesis and academic integrity checks:** confirm languages and formats for the main text, abstracts, forms and appendices. ### Explain language, resource and academic difficulties early

#### Make feedback understandable and actionable

Send brief materials and specific questions before meetings. Afterwards, record decisions, actions, owners and dates in a language everyone involved understands, and ask them to check the account. Listening comprehension or spoken fluency is not a measure of research ability. Say when written feedback, further explanation or a demonstration would help.

#### Agree on terminology and check translations

Map terms such as proposal review, external review and degree award to their official institutional names, English explanations and required documents. Check translations and AI-assisted text yourself. Ask the issuing office about ambiguous formal documents, and use only approved tools for unpublished research and personal information.

#### Test resource access in advance

Open required resources using your own account. If access fails, record the service, error and effect on the task, then ask university support about available channels or authorised alternatives. An installed application does not establish account access, a licence or a reliable service.

#### Seek support and request review

If study, health, communication or resource difficulties affect progress, contact your supervisor and the university's published academic or student support services to agree on adjustments and a review date. For concerns about records, supervision or assessment, retain facts and documents, use the published feedback, review or appeal channels, and record receipts. Share personal information only with those who need it.

Agree on support according to individual needs. Do not infer language ability, study habits or research skills from nationality. Discuss language improvements separately from gaps in research evidence so that the next steps remain clear and achievable.

## Research tools guide

## Research tools and task methods

Start with the smallest set of tools that can produce evidence for your task; add tools only to address a specific need. These steps are general research guidance. Check software features, institutional templates and service conditions against the relevant version and official documentation.

The recommended combination is a starting point for the task. Alternatives accommodate established habits or specific requirements. Tools marked as requiring preparation need an environment, access and budget check first. You do not need to install every tool in this catalogue.

### Literature search and review

Move from having read papers to being able to compare their questions, methods, evidence and limitations.

**Minimal starting combination:**Zotero + Excel + VS Code

**Steps**

1. Record the research question, search terms, databases, search date and inclusion/exclusion criteria first. Collect references by topic in Zotero, checking the metadata and full text of each paper.
2. Write a structured note for each core paper: question, study object, method, data, evaluation, findings and limitations. Give a page or section reference for important claims.
3. Build a review matrix in Excel or CSV, with one paper per row. Include task, dataset size, baselines, metrics, reproducibility materials, evidence strength and relevance to your study.
4. Organize the review around what is known, the limits of that evidence and what still needs testing. Use the Zotero citation plugin in Word; for LaTeX, export BibTeX / BibLaTeX and check citation keys.

**Delivery formats**

- literature-matrix.xlsx or .csv, including at least source identifiers, questions, methods, evidence and limitations.
- reading-notes.md, search-log.md, references.bib / .ris exports as needed, and the written review.

**Completion checks**

- Sample important claims and trace each to a specific location in the original paper. Distinguish the authors' findings from your own interpretation.
- The matrix explains why studies are or are not comparable; it is more than a collection of pasted abstracts.
- Adding a citation correctly updates both the text and bibliography. Attachment access complies with the source's permissions.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- For a simple matrix, use CSV + VS Code. If you already have an effective reading-note workflow, continue with Obsidian or OneNote.
- Learn Zotero's built-in export and Word plugin first. Assess third-party extensions only if automatic LaTeX citation-key management is needed; they are optional.

**Common mistakes**

- Importing a PDF does not mean its reference has been checked. Verify AI-generated authors, DOIs and claims against the original source.
- Markdown is useful for recording and exchanging text. Renaming an .md extension does not create a Word document that meets institutional formatting requirements.

Related templates: [Paper reading and reproduction decision](https://csma-graduation-projects.github.io/en/templates.html#reading); [Literature review and evidence matrix](https://csma-graduation-projects.github.io/en/templates.html#literature); [Topic justification and scope](https://csma-graduation-projects.github.io/en/templates.html#topic)

Documentation: [Zotero: getting started, items and notes](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide); [Zotero: Word plugin and citation updates](https://www.zotero.org/support/word_processor_plugin_usage); [Zotero: bibliographies and BibTeX / RIS export](https://www.zotero.org/support/creating_bibliographies)

### Empirical replication, algorithm experiments and data analysis

Connect every table and conclusion to its inputs, versions, execution records and calculations.

**Minimal starting combination:**VS Code + Git + Python + venv

**Steps**

1. Define the replication target, data license, metrics, baselines and acceptable differences first. Record the authors' reported values, preserve original data and document how it was obtained.
2. Choose an isolated Python environment and confirm its interpreter in VS Code. Record the operating system, Python, dependencies, hardware, random seeds and full commands. Run a small example before scaling up.
3. Specify cleaning, splitting, execution, evaluation and plotting as explicit steps. Assign each run an identifier and save its configuration, input identifiers, exit status, full logs and raw outputs.
4. Recalculate metrics from saved raw outputs using scripts. Report variation across repeated runs, failures and limitations. Algorithm comparisons must also control input size, resource conditions and the scope of timing.
5. Rerun a minimal experiment in a new output directory by following the README. Check that the record supports the conclusion; do not describe offline recalculation as a new live execution.

**Delivery formats**

- README.md, requirements.txt or the project's dependency lockfile, scripts, Git commit identifier and environment record.
- run-manifest.json, config.json, full logs, raw .jsonl / .csv results, analysis scripts and .svg / .pdf figures.

**Completion checks**

- Another student can locate the inputs and rerun the minimal example using the documented commands. Failures also have exit codes and records.
- Thesis tables can be calculated from saved raw results, and any manual adjustments are explicitly traceable.
- Check for data leakage, overlapping splits, inconsistent evaluation definitions and inappropriate exception handling or statistical methods.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- Excel is suitable for small preliminary checks. Preserve formulas and parameters for repeated calculations used in a thesis; move complex or batch analysis into scripts.
- Prepare containers, special hardware or a specific operating system when the paper explicitly requires them. If using notebooks for exploration, clear outputs and rerun all cells in order before delivery.
- Understand a statistical method's assumptions and applicability before choosing SciPy or another library. Do not apply a test simply because a button makes it available.

**Common mistakes**

- pip freeze records only part of the Python dependency information. It does not fully describe the operating system, GPU, drivers, data or model environment.
- Screenshots, progress bars, successful installation and one passing example cannot replace complete experimental results.

### LLM, agent and RAG research

Validate the task and evaluation first, then control models, data, tools and costs during experiments.

**Minimal starting combination:**VS Code + Git + Python + venv

**Steps**

1. Specify the task, data scope, baselines and definitions of success and failure. Distinguish answer quality, retrieval quality and end-to-end task completion. Start with a small sample that can be checked manually.
2. Use authorized models or services. Record model identifiers, versions or access dates, prompts, parameters, data snapshots, tool configuration and budget. Begin with a few real requests to verify the setup.
3. For RAG, preserve document sources, chunking rules, retrieved candidates and citation locations before evaluating answers. For agents, record each step's input, tool calls, return values, termination reason and actual environment state.
4. Keep actual model executions, offline replays, hand-constructed examples and failures separately identifiable. Evaluation scripts must inspect raw traces; a model's claim that a task is complete is insufficient.
5. Check data separation, baseline fairness, variation across runs, costs and error types. Without actual model access, deliver only a clearly labeled offline demonstration and a list of items awaiting validation.

**Delivery formats**

- Task and evaluation specifications, permission statements, data-split identifiers, prompts and model configuration.
- Per-request responses or de-identified .jsonl traces, tool execution records, cost and timing tables, evaluation code and failure examples.

**Completion checks**

- You can identify which actual execution produced a result and locate its data, code and model configuration.
- RAG citations trace to source passages. Agent completion is supported by the actual task state or an independent check.
- Resource conditions and evaluation definitions are documented for both baselines and the new method. Hand-constructed examples remain distinct from results on actual data.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- For a small RAG study, validate evaluation with simple retrieval and a file index before deciding whether a vector database is needed.
- AI assistants can help explain and draft material, but model services, GPUs, Docker, frameworks and accounts must be prepared and verified for the specific task.

**Common mistakes**

- Having a chat application or editor extension does not establish access to an experimental API, GPU, model or publishable data.
- Never describe cached replay, manually completed traces or simulated responses as live model experiments. Do not send unauthorized material to external services.

### Thesis writing, compliance checks and review submission

Keep the main manuscript, references, figures, revision responses and final review version consistent.

**Minimal starting combination:**Word + Zotero citation plugin + Zotero + draw.io

**Steps**

1. Download the institutional template applicable to you and choose a primary manuscript format. In Word, use styles and the citation plugin. If permitted and familiar, use LaTeX + VS Code instead.
2. Organize chapters around research questions, methods, evidence and limitations. Generate figures from actual results and preserve editable sources; align captions, units and references with the text.
3. Keep a version and response table for each revision round. Use tracked changes and comments in Word, or Git diffs and build logs in LaTeX, while maintaining one primary manuscript.
4. Follow applicable institutional requirements for formatting, similarity screening, non-academic-error checks and AI-use disclosure. Confirm what your institution means by non-academic-error checks, which are distinct from similarity or AI screening. For anonymous review, create a separate copy and check the cover, acknowledgements, self-citations, filename, properties, comments, tracked changes, attachments and links.
5. Export PDF from the final review copy. Check fonts, equations, page breaks, cross-references and anonymity page by page. Record the submitted version, date and receipt.

**Delivery formats**

- The primary .docx, or .tex + .bib + figure sources, together with the matching final .pdf.
- Revision-response table, formatting and anonymity checklists, applicable policy sources, reports you are permitted to retain, and submission receipts.

**Completion checks**

- The PDF matches the registered manuscript version, figures match raw results, and every citation can be located.
- Hiding markup in Word does not delete revisions. Handle tracked changes and comments as required in the review copy, then check it again.
- Word for Mac does not include the Windows Document Inspector. Check properties and manually inspect the text, comments, revisions and exported PDF; one button cannot establish complete anonymity.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- Choose Word or LaTeX as the primary format. After conversion, review the entire converted document against the requirements rather than maintaining two parallel manuscripts.
- Markdown is suitable for outlines and reading notes. Formatting still needs verification after transfer to the institutional Word / LaTeX template.

**Common mistakes**

- Use the screening system and thresholds required by your institution. A generic AI tool or commercial website score cannot substitute for them.
- Do not remove citation fields, comments or identifying information from the only manuscript copy. Retain both a controlled original and a review copy.

### Competition teamwork, proposal presentations and thesis defenses

Connect team responsibilities, evidence and presentation so that the package is ready to submit and the demonstration runs reliably.

**Minimal starting combination:**PowerPoint / Keynote + Git + draw.io

**Steps**

1. Maintain a task list with an owner, completion condition, review date and delivery location for each item. For competitions, first record the official rules, their version and submission requirements.
2. Use draw.io to clarify the system and method. In PowerPoint or Keynote, explain the problem, approach, evidence, contribution and limitations. Identify the evidence source on each results slide.
3. Prepare real demonstration inputs, key operations and fallback plans for the current code version. Record team contributions, material sources and any necessary ownership agreements.
4. Rehearse within the formal time limit, testing fonts, projection, audio, video and network dependencies on the actual equipment. Save a PDF, offline demonstration materials and anticipated questions with responses.
5. Before submitting, verify file formats, sizes, names and versions. Keep the receipt, then turn competition or defense feedback into action items.

**Delivery formats**

- The .pptx or .key source, backup .pdf, demonstration script, project version and a demonstration video that may lawfully be shared.
- Responsibility table, questions and answers, revision record, contribution statement and formal submission receipt.

**Completion checks**

- Test on another target device. Key steps do not depend on last-minute login or unverified permissions.
- The presentation distinguishes individual contributions, team contributions and reused work. Claims about the project match what is demonstrated.
- If the network or a service fails, state which features were not verified live. Label a backup video as a recording.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- Continue using Keynote if familiar. If the recipient requires PPTX, export early and rehearse every slide.
- Tencent Meeting can support remote discussion. Boardmix / EdrawMind can support brainstorming; record agreed conclusions in deliverable documents and task records.

**Common mistakes**

- Attractive slides and demonstration videos do not replace a runnable project or actual experiments.
- Successful competition registration, an internal rehearsal and formal institutional approval of a proposal or defense are different states.

### Routine management, data archiving and handover

Make work traceable, materials findable and results reviewable, with a usable handover for continued work.

**Minimal starting combination:**VS Code + Git + Excel

**Steps**

1. Maintain goals, owners, status, dates and evidence links in one consistent task table. Use the weekly-report and meeting templates, and have relevant participants confirm important decisions.
2. Keep code and text in version control. Store raw data, models, original manuscripts and sensitive materials in controlled storage with appropriate permissions. Distinguish original material, processed outputs and formally released versions.
3. When archiving, document the directory structure, data dictionary, source licenses, environment and execution steps. Record versions, file inventories and necessary checksums. Links must point to locations the recipient is authorized to access.
4. Use a separate backup location or an organization-approved backup service, and test restoration. At handover, the recipient should open the materials, rerun a minimal example, and confirm access and outstanding tasks.

**Delivery formats**

- weekly.md, meeting.md or corresponding Word records, a task table and an index of stage evidence.
- README.md, file inventory, data dictionary, checksum list, controlled backup record and handover confirmation.

**Completion checks**

- A sampled conclusion can be traced to original material, processing scripts and current outputs. Broken links have been repaired.
- Backups can be restored and recipients actually have the necessary access. Transfer permissions or accounts through the organization's authorized process; do not share personal account passwords.
- Public materials contain no personal information, accounts, credentials or unauthorized data. Restricted materials retain necessary provenance and permission notes.

**When to use alternatives or add tools**

- Use Obsidian / OneNote for daily notes and Excel for simple plans if helpful. Continue using an established team platform instead of duplicating status tables.
- EdrawProj can support more complex schedules. Add tools when dependencies across teams justify them; formal approvals still use the institution's designated channels.

**Common mistakes**

- Git commits, cloud synchronization and complete backups serve different purposes. Deletions may synchronize to remote storage, so verify the restoration route.
- Git LFS stores pointers and external large-file content. Service quotas, access and backups still need planning; it is not unconditional protection for data.

### A focused tool catalogue

- **Zotero (Recommended combination)**: Collect and verify reference metadata, link full texts, notes and citations, and trace each claim in your review to a specific source. Check authors, title, year, DOI and item type first. Automatically imported metadata is not guaranteed to be correct.

- **VS Code (Recommended combination)**: Edit code, Markdown and configuration files, select the project interpreter, and run and debug tests. Python, Markdown, Git and LaTeX can work together in one workspace. An installed extension does not establish that the project's interpreter, compiler or tests are correctly configured.
  Feature documentation: [VS Code: Python environments, debugging and testing](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/languages/python)
- **Git (Recommended combination)**: Save code and text changes in small, explainable units of work; use diffs and commit identifiers to trace results. Git manages versions. Large datasets, model weights and complete backups need separate controlled storage and tested restoration procedures.
  Feature documentation: [Git: recording changes and reviewing diffs](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Basics-Recording-Changes-to-the-Repository); [GitHub: limits and responsibilities of large-file storage](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/working-with-files/managing-large-files/about-git-large-file-storage)
- **Python + venv (Recommended combination)**: Use scripts to process data, run experiments, calculate metrics and generate figures. Select NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, SciPy or pytest according to the task. Record the interpreter, dependencies, operating system and commands for each project. You do not need to install every library to begin a small exercise.
  Feature documentation: [Python: isolated virtual environments and recreation](https://docs.python.org/3/library/venv.html); [VS Code: Python environments, debugging and testing](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/languages/python)
- **Excel (Recommended combination)**: Build literature comparison matrices, task tables and data dictionaries. Preserve formulas, inputs and analysis parameters when using spreadsheets for small checks or analyses. Screenshots and manually edited values cannot replace reproducible analysis. Put repeated cleaning, statistics and plotting in scripts, and preserve the raw data.
  Feature documentation: [Excel: Analysis ToolPak](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/load-the-analysis-toolpak-in-excel)
- **draw.io (Recommended combination)**: Draw workflows, system architectures and experimental designs. Preserve editable .drawio source files and export SVG or PDF copies. Check text, arrows, font sizes and cropping after export. An image without its source file is difficult to revise later.
  Feature documentation: [draw.io: source files and SVG / PDF export](https://www.drawio.com/docs/manual/export/export-diagram/)
- **Word + Zotero citation plugin (Recommended combination)**: Write and revise collaboratively in an institution-approved Word template, using styles, captions, cross-references and automatically updated bibliographies. Verify the plugin and template first. Hiding tracked changes does not remove them. Anonymization procedures differ between Mac and Windows.
  Feature documentation: [Zotero: Word plugin and citation updates](https://www.zotero.org/support/word_processor_plugin_usage); [Word: tracked changes versus hidden markup](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/word/training/track-changes-in-word); [Word: Document Inspector and differences on Mac](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/inspect-document-b0088a7a-d482-4b87-b762-7c94c7c71e23)

Rehearse on the actual presentation equipment. After converting between applications, check fonts, equations, video and animations on every slide.

- **Obsidian / OneNote (Optional alternative)**: Record reading ideas, meetings and work logs. Obsidian's Markdown files can connect notes with code documentation. Choose one note system that you can maintain over time. Important decisions and formal submissions must still enter the agreed task record or institutional system.
