International Student Guide

Clarify applicable requirements, language arrangements and support at each stage of undergraduate, master’s and doctoral study.

General guidance · Formal requirements follow the institutional rules applicable to you

This guide is for international students pursuing a bachelor's, master's or doctoral degree at a university in China. It helps you connect programme requirements with coursework, research and degree applications. For exchange, visiting or joint programmes, first confirm the agreement on enrolment, credit recognition, supervision and the awarding institution, then use the relevant sections.

EN / ZH changes the page language. It does not determine student status or change programme requirements. Academic integrity, research quality and accurate records matter for everyone; specific courses, assessments and achievement requirements depend on your entry cohort, discipline, degree type and programme. Studying in English does not automatically make English the approved language for every procedure, thesis document or defence.

For cooperation between institutions, start with the Joint Training guide and then use this page for applicable language, registration, mobility and support arrangements.

Confirm your requirements before planning each stage

When you join the group, review the requirements with your supervisor and the relevant school or department staff. The process below is available directly on this page. Keep a record for each step; a verbal impression alone does not establish completion.

  1. Confirm your programmeRecord your enrolment status, entry cohort, degree type, applicable document versions and responsible offices.
  2. Confirm languages and proceduresCheck the languages used for courses, communication, documents and assessments, and record official contacts and dates for required procedures.
  3. Choose your degree pathwaySelect the undergraduate, master's or doctoral pathway and identify completed, current and upcoming formal stages.
  4. Agree on tasks and deliverablesSpecify the task, materials, completion criteria, feedback method and review date.
  5. Complete formal reviews and revisionsRetain official decisions and assign actions, owners and follow-up dates to incomplete or unsuccessful items.
  6. Complete award and departure recordsCheck degree status, final documents, data permissions, resource returns and departure records separately.

Return to verification when a requirement is unclear: mark it “Pending confirmation” and name who will ask, which office will answer and when confirmation is expected. Missing documents, language approval or formal decisions do not count as a pass. For an inapplicable item, record the reason and supporting confirmation.

Undergraduate: distinguish research training, capstone work and competitions

These activities have different purposes and recognition procedures. Research training develops research methods, while competitions offer practice in team delivery. Whether either counts towards a course, practice credits or a capstone project requires confirmation under your programme's rules.

  • Research training: check participation criteria, prerequisite knowledge, supervision and permitted data. Begin with a manageable question and submit reading notes, experiment or implementation records, and a short presentation. Record the task scope, your contribution, feedback and next review date.
  • Courses and credits: confirm course selection, teaching and assessment languages, required courses and credit recognition. If a project needs formal recognition, retain the course mapping, application and official decision; do not assign yourself credits.
  • Capstone project: check topic approval, the task brief, proposal, progress reviews, thesis format, abstracts, assessment and defence languages in advance. Retain the approved topic, stage documents, revisions and formal results. A group presentation does not complete the university's capstone procedures.
  • Competitions: before registering, ask the organiser about eligibility for international students and your year of study, team rules, submission languages, public presentation and use of outputs. Record the rules version, eligibility confirmation, responsibilities and submission receipt. A prize does not automatically replace graduation requirements.
  • Completion and next steps: organise reproducible code or experimental materials, documentation and your reflection; distinguish public work from restricted materials. Record what remains for credit recognition, the capstone project or further research.

Master's: align research progress with formal programme stages

Confirm whether you are pursuing an academic or professional degree, the applicable programme requirements and any practice component before making your timeline. The same research task may support different formal documents. Record supervisory feedback separately from institutional decisions.

  • Topic and literature review: develop a topic statement covering the question, prior work, available data and feasible scope. Agree on languages for reading discussions, group meetings and written feedback; retain the bibliography, problem evidence and supervisor's comments.
  • Proposal and midterm review: use the applicable documents to check prerequisites, formats, presentation languages, timing and responsible offices. Submit the report, evidence and plan, then retain official comments, responses and follow-up decisions. A group rehearsal is not a formal pass.
  • Research outputs and practice: check required research achievements, practice and academic activities item by item. Record the applicable rule, recognition criteria, your contribution, available evidence and missing items. Do not borrow numerical requirements from another cohort or degree type.
  • Thesis and academic integrity checks: confirm languages and formats for the main text, abstracts, forms and appendices. Ask the responsible office what the locally used term “chafei” means, what it checks and how it is handled. Record similarity screening and any other integrity checks separately, including requirements, reports and corrections; do not assume these checks are interchangeable.
  • External review and defence: confirm review-document languages, anonymisation, submission eligibility, the defence language and required approvals in advance. Retain the submitted version, review comments, responses, defence decision and final revisions. Permission to write in a foreign language does not itself authorise a defence in that language.
  • Degree application and archive: check the application status, final thesis and attachments, and permissions for data and code. Retain official receipts and complete the handover. Track “defence completed”, “degree awarded” and “departure completed” as separate states.

Explain language, resource and academic difficulties early

Make feedback understandable and actionable

Send brief materials and specific questions before meetings. Afterwards, record decisions, actions, owners and dates in a language everyone involved understands, and ask them to check the account. Listening comprehension or spoken fluency is not a measure of research ability. Say when written feedback, further explanation or a demonstration would help.

Agree on terminology and check translations

Map terms such as proposal review, external review and degree award to their official institutional names, English explanations and required documents. Check translations and AI-assisted text yourself. Ask the issuing office about ambiguous formal documents, and use only approved tools for unpublished research and personal information.

Test resource access in advance

Open required resources using your own account. If access fails, record the service, error and effect on the task, then ask university support about available channels or authorised alternatives. An installed application does not establish account access, a licence or a reliable service.

Seek support and request review

If study, health, communication or resource difficulties affect progress, contact your supervisor and the university's published academic or student support services to agree on adjustments and a review date. For concerns about records, supervision or assessment, retain facts and documents, use the published feedback, review or appeal channels, and record receipts. Share personal information only with those who need it.

Agree on support according to individual needs. Do not infer language ability, study habits or research skills from nationality. Discuss language improvements separately from gaps in research evidence so that the next steps remain clear and achievable.

Public sources and how to use this guide

Public sources checked: 19 October 2026. The notes below summarise national-level provisions. The other workflows, recordkeeping methods and communication suggestions on this page are practical guidance, not institutional regulations.

  • Administrative Measures for the Enrolment and Education of International Students in Schools (Chinese source), Articles 16 and 19: degree programmes include required Chinese language and China overview courses. Students educated in a foreign language may write their thesis in that language, with a Chinese abstract. The institution determines whether the defence may use a foreign language.
  • Ministry of Education Q&A on international student education, 2019 (Chinese source): stresses educational quality, shared quality assurance and equal teaching resources, alongside reasonable academic support that takes language and cultural differences into account.

This portal does not identify the admissions or programme documents applicable to your institution, school and entry cohort. Obtain the current versions, notices and contacts through official university channels and record them in the checklist. If documents conflict or Chinese and English wording is unclear, seek an explicit answer from the issuing or responsible office before making the related submission. Do not infer eligibility, exemptions or approval from this guide.
