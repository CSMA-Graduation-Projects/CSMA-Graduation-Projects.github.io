Doctoral Research Guidance

Doctoral study develops a sustained research direction, contributions to questions at the frontier of a field, high-quality outputs and lasting capabilities in writing, systems, experimentation and research infrastructure.

Materials and applicability: This page gives public research guidance. It does not store individual assessments, supervisor evaluations, unpublished papers or data, collaboration materials, server details or account credentials; working records belong in approved private systems. Program components, their order, output requirements and timing follow the university and department rules applicable to you. International students should not infer their degree procedures or eligibility from this overview. Start with the local requirements check and use the general templates where appropriate.

The purpose of doctoral study

Investigate frontier questions and make sustained contributions

Develop a coherent research direction connected to the group's long-term interests. Build a deep understanding of current work, identify distinctive questions and approaches, and preserve contributions through papers, systems, projects, data and research infrastructure that others can build on.

Work towards high-quality research outputs

  • Build a sustained line of work around an important question rather than completing disconnected tasks.
  • Attend to problem definition, theoretical or methodological contributions, credible experiments and interpretation of results.
  • Produce work that can be examined, such as research papers, project outputs, systems or data resources.
  • Make methods, code, experimental procedures and writing materials reusable within the group.

Progress reporting and thesis preparation

  • Report progress regularly, explaining research developments, key difficulties, experiment status and the next plan.
  • Retain a complete record for important papers, from the question and method through experiments to submission versions.
  • The thesis should present a clear line of argument, a coherent set of contributions, sufficient experiments and supporting outputs.
  • Maintain material that can support applicable qualification reviews, midterm assessments, preliminary defenses and the final defense.

Projects, systems and papers

Preserve individual work for future research

Project records

Retain project goals, task breakdowns, milestone outputs, data sources, collaboration interfaces and acceptance materials so future members can continue the work.

System documentation

Maintain the architecture, core modules, runtime environment, deployment instructions, test data and known issues so the system remains runnable and maintainable.

Paper records

Organise manuscript versions, experiment records, figure and table sources, reviewer feedback, revisions and final publication materials to preserve the full research record.

Experiment records

Save configurations, data processing steps, parameter settings, results and reproduction instructions to support later verification and extension.

Maintain working records

Keep research logs, progress reports, paper materials, project documents and experiment records throughout doctoral study. These should support problem tracking, examination of outputs, thesis writing and graduation review.

  • Retain key decisions, unsuccessful experiments, reasons for changes and milestone conclusions.
  • Keep externally submitted material consistent with the repository and experiment records.
  • Document important outputs so others can use them, rather than retaining only personal local notes.

Prepare a usable handover

Before graduating or leaving the group for a period, document the research direction, project materials, systems, data and unfinished work for the recipient.

  • Identify questions that can be pursued, code entry points, experimental environments and risks.
  • Describe the management status of accounts, servers, data, platforms and collaborations. Public material should give only non-sensitive status information. Transfer specific access details, private contacts and organisation-resource ownership or permissions through approved platform procedures; do not hand over personal passwords.
  • Provide recommendations for future maintenance and instructions for citing the work.
